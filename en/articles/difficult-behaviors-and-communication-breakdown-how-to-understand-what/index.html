Difficult behaviors and communication breakdown: how to understand what happens

A crisis, refusal or behavior that worries adults does not automatically reveal its cause. When communicating is difficult, behavior may be a way of expressing discomfort or escaping a situation, but it is essential to also assess pain, health, demands and environment.

Investigate before trying to control

Observe what happens before, during and after: noise, change, waiting, difficult task, hunger, pain, sleep or inability to ask for a break. Ask what function the response might be fulfilling without assuming manipulation. Sudden changes or physical signs warrant health assessment.

Teach alternatives and make ordering accessible

Provide simple ways to ask for help, pause, finish, choose or decline, including AAC if used. Anticipate change, adjust the task, and respond predictably. Interventions should be based on functional assessment, protect dignity and assent, and teach useful skills, not punish communication.

In short

  • Look for physical, communicative, and environmental causes before defining a strategy.
  • Supporting functional communication and adjusting barriers can prevent distress.

In summary

Behavior is information for the team; Understanding this information helps you respond safely and respectfully.

Informative content. It does not replace individual assessment or clinical or educational guidance.

Frequently asked questions

Does this theme apply the same to all children?

No. The decision must consider development, preferences, context and individual assessment.

When should I ask for help?

When the difficulty persists, affects communication, learning, safety or well-being, talk to qualified professionals and the school, depending on the context.

Does this information replace an individual assessment?

No. This article is general information. Decisions should be based on the child’s needs, context and assessment by qualified professionals.

Where can families find reliable guidance?

Start with the official sources linked in this article and discuss the child’s situation with the school and relevant qualified professionals.

This article is for information only and does not replace an assessment by a health professional. If you are worried about a child’s development, talk to their doctor or paediatrician.
