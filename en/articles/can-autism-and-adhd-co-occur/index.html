Myth or fact: can autism and ADHD co-occur?

Myth: an autism diagnosis rules out ADHD, or vice versa. The two conditions can co-occur. If additional concerns arise, assess them without assuming everything is explained by the known diagnosis.

By the Talk+ team 18 October 2026 3 min read
Loading reads…

Why can some signs overlap?

Attention, social communication, regulation, sleep and executive functioning can raise questions about more than one condition. The appearance of a behaviour does not reveal its cause by itself. The team considers history, settings and impact before assigning clinical meaning.

Does an earlier diagnosis close the assessment?

No. NICE says autism assessment should consider coexisting conditions and lists ADHD among differential diagnoses; guidance also warns against ruling out autism assessment because someone has an ADHD diagnosis. It may help to ask whether newer difficulties are persistent and occur in different settings.

What information may help the team?

Describe each concern separately: when it happens, what task was under way, what helps and how it affects participation or wellbeing. Home and school reports may complement assessment without replacing clinical evaluation. Ask which possibilities were considered and why.

In short

  • Autism and ADHD can co-occur; one diagnosis does not automatically rule out the other.
  • Similar behaviours need to be understood through history, context and impact.

Conclusion

Avoid attributing every need to the first label received. If new questions arise, bring them to the team and ask explicitly about co-occurrence and other explanations.
