Speech delay at 3 years: short sentences, unclear speech

At 3, most children can hold a conversation: they ask questions, say what happened and make themselves understood by people outside the family. When a child still uses single words or is very hard to understand, it is time to ask for an assessment.

Speech delay at 3 years can show up in different ways: few words, very short sentences, difficulty understanding or speech nobody can follow. In this article we explain what is expected at this age and what to do. See also Speech delay: warning signs.

In short

  • At 3, many children hold back-and-forth conversations, ask questions and use sentences of three or more words.
  • People outside the family usually understand most of what the child says.
  • Single words, two-word phrases or very unclear speech at 3 mean an assessment is needed.
  • At this age, the earlier support starts, the more time there is before school.

What is expected at 3 years?

According to milestones revised by the American Academy of Pediatrics, by age 3 most children keep up a conversation with at least two back-and-forth exchanges, ask “who”, “what”, “where” or “why” questions, say what is happening in a picture and talk well enough for others to understand them most of the time (Zubler and colleagues, 2022).

  • SentencesJoins three or more words: “The dog is eating.”
  • Questions“Where’s Daddy?”, “What’s that?”
  • ClarityFamily and outsiders understand most of what they say, even with some sound errors.
  • UnderstandingUnderstands “on”, “in”, “big” and two-step instructions.

Warning signs at 3

Ask for an assessment if, at 3, the child

  • mostly uses single words or two-word phrases;
  • does not ask questions or say what happened;
  • is very hard to understand, even for family;
  • does not understand simple instructions or answers off-topic;
  • often gets frustrated at not being understood.

When speech is unclear, a speech and language therapist checks whether the sound errors are typical for the age or a speech sound disorder (see Speech sound errors). If words and sentences are the main issue, they assess language (see Speech delay and DLD).

How to help at home and at nursery

  • Expand what the child says: if they say “blue car”, reply “Yes, the blue car is going fast!”.
  • Ask open questions at calm moments: “What did you do at the park?”.
  • Do not ask them to repeat mispronounced words; model the right word in your reply.
  • Share with nursery staff what works at home and ask for their observations.
  • Read together and tell stories with pictures, inviting the child to tell them too.

Frequently asked questions about speech delay at 3 years

The 3-year-old is not making sentences. Is it a speech delay?

At 3, many children join three or more words. If they mostly use single words, it is worth asking for an assessment.

Nobody understands what the 3-year-old says. Is that normal?

Some sound errors are normal, but outsiders should understand most of it. If they do not, ask for an assessment.

Is it still early enough at 3?

Yes. Intervention can help at any age, and starting before school gives more time to progress.

Can speech delay at 3 be autism?

It can have several causes. If there are also differences in interaction and interests, the assessment should cover that too.

This article is for information only and does not replace an assessment by a health professional. If you are worried about a child’s development, talk to their doctor or paediatrician.
