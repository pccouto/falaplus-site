Pregnancy and language development: folic acid, iodine and valproate

Some choices in pregnancy are linked to a child's neurodevelopment. It helps to know what is well established, what is only an association and what should never be a reason for guilt.

By the Talk+ team 5 November 2026 4 min read
Loading reads…

This article is for people planning a pregnancy or who are pregnant, and for families asking «was it something in the pregnancy?». We cover folic acid in pregnancy and autism, iodine and the epilepsy medicine valproate.

In short

  • Folic acid before and in early pregnancy prevents neural tube defects; a large Norwegian study also linked it to fewer autism diagnoses, but that is an association.
  • Mild iodine deficiency in pregnancy was linked to lower verbal and reading scores in children.
  • Valproate in pregnancy increases the risk of birth defects and neurodevelopmental disorders, including language delay and autism.
  • Never stop a medicine on your own: talk to a doctor before getting pregnant.

Does folic acid in pregnancy reduce the risk of autism?

The main reason to take folic acid is well known: preventing neural tube defects. The WHO recommends a daily supplement from before conception to the end of the first trimester. In a Norwegian study of about 85,000 children, mothers who took folic acid from 4 weeks before to 8 weeks after conception had fewer children diagnosed with autistic disorder (Surén et al., 2013). This is an association: it does not prove cause, and many autistic children were born after pregnancies with folic acid.

Why does iodine matter in pregnancy?

Iodine is needed for thyroid hormones, which are essential for the baby's brain development. In the UK ALSPAC study, children of mothers with mild to moderate iodine deficiency in pregnancy had, on average, lower verbal IQ and reading scores (Bath et al., 2013). Recommendations on iodine supplements differ between countries (for example, the United States and the United Kingdom give different advice), so follow your national guidance and your doctor or midwife.

Can valproate in pregnancy affect a child's language?

Valproate is an effective medicine for epilepsy and bipolar disorder. In pregnancy, it increases the risk of birth defects and neurodevelopmental disorders: speech and language delay, learning difficulties and autism. In a Danish population study, exposure to valproate in pregnancy was linked to a higher risk of autism (Christensen et al., 2013). In 2018 the European Medicines Agency restricted its use in girls and women who can become pregnant, with a pregnancy prevention programme; the UK and other regulators have similar measures.

If you take valproate or another epilepsy medicine:

  • do not stop it on your own: a seizure in pregnancy is also dangerous;
  • talk to your doctor before getting pregnant so the medication can be reviewed;
  • if you are already pregnant, contact your doctor urgently.

Was it something I did in pregnancy?

For the vast majority of children there is no single identifiable cause of autism or language delay. Genetics plays an important role. This information is for planning future pregnancies, not for assigning blame. What matters now is supporting the child. Vaccines, for example, do not cause autism: see Vaccines and autism.

What we know

  • Folic acid prevents neural tube defects.
  • Iodine is needed for the baby's brain development.
  • Valproate in pregnancy increases the risk of neurodevelopmental disorders.

What cannot be said

  • «Folic acid prevents autism.» It is an association.
  • «The child's autism was the mother's fault.»
  • «More supplements in pregnancy are always better.»

Frequently asked questions about pregnancy and neurodevelopment

Does folic acid prevent autism?

A large study linked folic acid in early pregnancy to fewer autism diagnoses, but it is an association, not a guarantee.

Which medicines in pregnancy are linked to autism?

Valproate has the strongest evidence. Any medicine in pregnancy should be reviewed with a doctor, without stopping it on your own.

Do I need iodine in pregnancy?

Advice differs between countries. Follow your national guidance and ask your doctor or midwife.

Can autism be caused by something the mother did in pregnancy?

In most cases there is no single identifiable cause and genetics plays an important role. It is not a reason for guilt.

This article is for information only and does not replace an assessment by a health professional. If you are worried about a child’s development, talk to their doctor or paediatrician.
