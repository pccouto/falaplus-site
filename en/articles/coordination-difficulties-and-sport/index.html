Coordination difficulties and children’s sport

Coordination difficulties and sport can meet when catching a ball, balancing or learning a sequence takes additional effort.

Coordination difficulties and sport can meet when catching a ball, balancing or learning a sequence takes additional effort.

How do coordination difficulties and sport overlap?

Developmental coordination disorder involves difficulties learning motor skills that affect daily life. A problem with one game does not establish this diagnosis: assessment and consideration of other causes are needed. (Canadian Paediatric Society)

The Canadian Paediatric Society recommends supporting participation and, where necessary, occupational therapy or physiotherapy. It highlights task-specific interventions. These recommendations do not make every sporting exercise a therapy. (Canadian Paediatric Society)

How can a task be adapted while keeping a challenge?

As a practical suggestion, try a larger, soft ball thrown over a short distance. Then change just one variable: distance, speed or movement. Explore with the child which version allows successful practice.

Before combining running, passing and opposition, a pass could be practised from a stable position. This example illustrates breaking a task down; it is not an individual rehabilitation prescription.

How can progress be reviewed and support sought?

Agree a goal chosen with the child, such as exchanging a few passes with a partner. Compare attempts with the child’s own starting point and retain meaningful opportunities to play.

Persistent difficulties affecting several daily tasks justify a conversation with health professionals. Recent loss of skills or new weakness needs assessment rather than being automatically attributed to coordination. (Canadian Paediatric Society)

What can we say — and what can we not say?

What we can say

  • Tasks can be adjusted and individual progress reviewed.

What we cannot say

  • Missing a pass diagnoses a disorder or measures intelligence.

Frequently asked questions

Does difficulty catching a ball mean dyspraxia?

No. One difficulty cannot establish a diagnosis. Development and daily functioning need to be considered.

What is the best sport for coordination difficulties?

There is no universal option. Interests, safety, adjustments and teaching quality should guide the choice.

Should children be excluded from team games?

They should not be excluded solely because of coordination difficulties. Adjust tasks and listen to their preferences.

When should assessment be sought?

When persistent difficulties interfere with daily tasks, learning or participation. Recent loss of skills warrants medical assessment.

This article is for information only and does not replace an assessment by a health professional. If you are worried about a child’s development, talk to their doctor or paediatrician.
