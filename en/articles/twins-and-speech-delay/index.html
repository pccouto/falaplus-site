Twins and speech delay: why it is more common

Parents of twins often hear that «twins talk later». Studies confirm that, on average, this is true at age 2, and also show that the gap tends to narrow with age.

By the Talk+ team 24 October 2026 3 min read
Loading reads…

The link between twins and speech delay has been known for decades as the «twinning effect». In this article we summarise what twin studies say, what explains the difference, when it narrows and how to support twin language development without waiting for it to «sort itself out».

In short

  • At 24 months, twins on average have lower language scores than single-born children.
  • Premature birth does not, on its own, explain the difference.
  • Studies found a heritable component in vocabulary and grammar.
  • The gap tends to narrow between ages 4 and 6, but a delay should be assessed as in any child.

Do twins talk later?

In an Australian study of 473 twin pairs, twins at 24 months had average language scores below the norms for single-born children. Identical twins scored lower than non-identical twins, and boys lower than girls (Rice et al., 2014).

The same study found significant heritability for vocabulary and grammar, and for late language emergence itself. The authors see this as contradicting the idea that the delay is only due to parents having less attention to give each child.

What explains the difference?

British studies by Rutter and Thorpe (2003) used twins as a «natural experiment» to study the causes of mild language delay. They concluded that premature birth is not the main cause of the delay in twins and also looked at family interaction factors.

  • Biological factorsGenetics, sex and pregnancy and birth conditions.
  • Three-way conversationsConversations are often shared: each child has fewer one-to-one moments with the adult.
  • Language between the twoTwins may communicate with simplified words only they understand.

Does the gap go away?

Follow-up of the same children showed that the gap narrows at ages 4 and 6. But an average says nothing about a particular child: a twin may have a language disorder that does not resolve on its own. That is why the warning signs are the same as for any child (see Speech delay: warning signs).

What we know

  • On average, twins start talking later.
  • There is a heritable component.
  • The gap tends to narrow with age.

What we cannot say

  • «Twins always talk late, no need to assess.»
  • «It’s only because parents have less time.»
  • «All twins catch up on their own.»

How to support each twin’s language

  • Set aside short one-to-one moments with each child: reading, playing or chatting at bath time.
  • Call each child by name and respond to each one, even when they talk at the same time.
  • Repeat the words they invent between themselves with the correct model, without harsh correction.
  • If one twin talks much less than the other, do not wait: ask for an assessment.

Frequently asked questions about twins and speech

Is it normal for twins to talk later?

On average, yes: at age 2 twins have lower language scores. Even so, a delay should be assessed.

Do identical twins talk later than fraternal twins?

In a study of 473 twin pairs, identical twins scored lower on average at 24 months.

Do twins have a secret language?

Many use simplified words between themselves. It is common and usually fades; what matters is that each child talks with adults.

When do twins catch up?

In studies, the gap narrows between ages 4 and 6. A child with warning signs should not wait until then.

This article is for information only and does not replace an assessment by a health professional. If you are worried about a child’s development, talk to their doctor or paediatrician.
