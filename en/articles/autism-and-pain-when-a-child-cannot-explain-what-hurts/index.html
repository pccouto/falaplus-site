Autism and pain: when a child cannot explain what hurts

Autism and pain: when a child cannot explain what hurts is a common search among families and professionals. A useful answer must recognise individual differences, explain limits and offer practical next steps without promising outcomes.

By the Talk+ team 30 October 2026 3 min read
Loading reads…

What is important to understand?

Pain may appear as crying, withdrawal, agitation, changes in sleep, eating, posture, movement or tolerance for activities. Individual patterns and change from usual matter.

What cautions are needed?

Do not assume change is “just autism”, sensory or behavioural. A child may feel and express pain differently; pain boards do not replace medical assessment.

What can you do next?

Teach ways to indicate location, intensity and yes/no using the body, photos, visual scales or AAC. Seek urgent care for severe pain, injury, breathing difficulty or concerning change.
