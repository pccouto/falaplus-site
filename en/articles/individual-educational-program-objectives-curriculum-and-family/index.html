Individual Educational Program: objectives, curriculum and family participation

The Individual Educational Program (PEI) is an instrument designed for students who benefit from significant curricular adaptations. It must clarify the educational path and support participation, learning and transition, with the involvement of the family and the multidisciplinary team.

When is it prepared?

DL 54/2018 provides for the PEI when significant curricular adaptations are mobilized, an additional measure that implies the introduction of substitute learning and/or global objectives in terms of knowledge to be acquired and skills to be developed. This decision should not be confused with small adaptations of materials, time or form of response.

The document should guide daily life

A useful IEP links objectives to the activities and contexts in which the student learns, indicates supports and forms of assessment, and is reviewed regularly. The family participates in the preparation and evaluation. The student must be able to express interests and choices. The team can discuss how to maintain access to the group and common experiences, even when there are individualized goals.

In short

  • The PEI applies within the specific legal framework of significant curricular adaptations.
  • It must be clear, accompanied and articulated with participation and transitions.

In summary

The family can ask for an accessible explanation of each objective and how it will be worked on in different routines and disciplines.

Informative content. It does not replace individual assessment or clinical or educational guidance.

Frequently asked questions

Does this theme apply the same to all children?

No. The decision must consider development, preferences, context and individual assessment.

When should I ask for help?

When the difficulty persists, affects communication, learning, safety or well-being, talk to qualified professionals and the school, depending on the context.

Does this information replace an individual assessment?

No. This article is general information. Decisions should be based on the child’s needs, context and assessment by qualified professionals.

Where can families find reliable guidance?

Start with the official sources linked in this article and discuss the child’s situation with the school and relevant qualified professionals.

This article is for information only and does not replace an assessment by a health professional. If you are worried about a child’s development, talk to their doctor or paediatrician.
