How is ADHD assessed in children?

An assessment for attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) asks whether symptoms are persistent, occur in more than one setting and affect functioning. A questionnaire alone does not establish diagnosis.

By the Talk+ team 18 October 2026 3 min read

What information is considered?

NICE recommends a full clinical and psychosocial assessment, developmental and psychiatric history, and information from observers. The team may consider family, school, other conditions, social circumstances and physical health, and include the child’s views where possible.

What are rating scales and school reports for?

Scales can organise reports and observations, but should not be the sole basis for diagnosis. School information may show how attention, impulsivity or activity vary across tasks; it is not a vote and does not replace clinical assessment. Differences between home and school need exploring.

How are criteria applied?

Diagnostic systems consider symptoms of inattention and/or hyperactivity-impulsivity, onset during childhood, presence in at least two important settings and functional impact. Counting and interpretation depend on the system and age. Only appropriately trained professionals should decide whether criteria apply.

In short

  • Diagnosis integrates history, functional impact and information from different settings.
  • Rating scales and school reports are useful sources, but do not diagnose by themselves.

Conclusion

Ask the report to explain which symptoms were observed, where, since when and with what impact. It should also note other explanations or conditions that may need assessment.
