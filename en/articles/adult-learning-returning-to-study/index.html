Adult learning: returning to study

Adult learning may begin with a career change, a long-standing interest or a task someone wants to do more independently.

Adult learning may begin with a career change, a long-standing interest or a task someone wants to do more independently.

What supports this approach?

The OECD describes adult learning as requiring flexible and accessible opportunities. Participation depends on time, resources and access as well as motivation. (OECD)

The Institute of Education Sciences guide brings together study organisation strategies, including distributed practice and checking learning. It does not establish an ideal timetable for each adult. (Institute of Education Sciences)

How can it be tried in practice?

The following steps are examples to adapt to the learner and context; they have not been evaluated as an intervention programme.

  1. Define an observable goal: writing a professional message, understanding a topic or completing a task.
  2. Try a small initial task to identify existing knowledge without turning the starting point into a personal judgement.
  3. Schedule realistic opportunities during the week and prepare an alternative for days with less time.
  4. Finish by explaining or applying what was learnt and choose the next step from the difficulties identified.

How can progress be reviewed?

Review what became clearer, what was applied and which barriers appeared. Adjust workload, format or support before concluding that the capacity to learn has been lost.

What can and cannot be concluded?

What we can say

  • Adult learning can be planned around individual goals and real circumstances.

What we cannot say

  • Adulthood prevents learning or a rigid plan suits everyone.

Frequently asked questions

Is it too late to return to study as an adult?

There is no universal deadline. Plans should consider goals, prior knowledge and individual circumstances.

Must I study every day?

There is no compulsory frequency. Aim for feasible continuity and opportunities to revisit and apply learning.

How can I tell whether the approach helps?

Define an observable task, record the help needed and revisit understanding or application later. Speed alone does not measure learning.

Do these suggestions replace individual assessment?

No. Review adjustments with the learner and relevant professionals when difficulties persist or concerning changes appear.

This article is for information only and does not replace an assessment by a health professional. If you are worried about a child’s development, talk to their doctor or paediatrician.
