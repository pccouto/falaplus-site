Language milestones from 0 to 24 months: what to expect

In the first two years, communication moves from crying to smiling, from cooing to pointing and from first words to first phrases. Milestones help follow this journey, without being a test.

By the Talk+ team 24 October 2026 3 min read
Loading reads…

Language milestones from 0 to 24 months are some of the most searched information by people caring for babies. In this article we summarise the reference points revised in 2022 by the CDC and the American Academy of Pediatrics, with signs that justify talking to a doctor. For later ages, see Language milestones from 2 to 5.

In short

  • In 2022, the CDC and AAP revised the milestones: each one reflects what 75% of children already do at that age.
  • So not reaching a milestone is a reason to talk to the doctor, not to wait.
  • Before words come smiling, cooing, babbling, gestures and pointing.
  • Developmental surveillance is part of routine child health visits.

Why were the milestones revised?

In 2022, an expert group from the CDC and the American Academy of Pediatrics revised the milestone checklists. The main change: each milestone now reflects what 75% of children already do at that age, instead of 50% (Zubler et al., 2022). So when a child does not reach a milestone, the recommendation is to talk to the doctor rather than wait and see.

What to expect at each age

AgeWhat most children already doTalk to the doctor if…
6 monthsTakes turns making sounds with you, like a conversation; laughs and squealsdoes not react to sounds or smile at people
12 monthsWaves «bye-bye»; calls a parent «mama» or «dada»; understands «no»does not point, wave or often respond to their name
18 monthsTries to say three or more words besides «mama» or «dada»; points to show; follows a one-step direction without gesturessays no words, does not point to show or has lost words
24 monthsSays at least two words together («more milk»); points to pictures in a book when askeddoes not combine two meaningful words (repeating does not count)

These reference points are based on the CDC and AAP checklists. They are surveillance reference points, not diagnoses: each child has their own pace, but loss of skills at any age should always be raised with the doctor.

Talk to the doctor, at any age, if the child:

  • has lost words, gestures or interest in people they already had;
  • does not react to sounds or seems not to hear;
  • does not try to communicate with looks, gestures or sounds.

How to encourage language in the first two years

  • Respond to the baby’s sounds as if they were conversation: they «talk», you answer, and wait.
  • Name what the child is looking at or pointing to: «It’s a dog!».
  • Sing, read picture books and play peekaboo.
  • Reduce background noise and screen time without interaction (see Screen time for children).

Frequently asked questions about baby language

When do babies start talking?

Around 12 months many babies say «mama» or «dada» with meaning; words keep growing through the second year.

How many words should an 18-month-old say?

According to the CDC and AAP checklists, at 18 months most children try to say three or more words besides «mama» or «dada».

Is it normal for a 12-month-old not to talk?

Many 12-month-olds say few or no words. Pointing, waving and responding to their name matter more; if they don’t, talk to the doctor.

My 18-month-old is not talking. Should I worry?

It is worth talking to the doctor, who will also check hearing, understanding and gestures.

This article is for information only and does not replace an assessment by a health professional. If you are worried about a child’s development, talk to their doctor or paediatrician.
