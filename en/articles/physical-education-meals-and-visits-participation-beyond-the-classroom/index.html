Physical education, meals and visits: participation beyond the classroom

School life doesn't just happen during one class. Noise in the cafeteria, unclear rules in a game, a change in routine during a visit or an inaccessible physical activity can create barriers. Planning participation with the student helps the school to anticipate needs without excluding them from the outset.

Identify the barrier before choosing the adaptation

Talk to the child and observe what makes participation difficult: quick instructions, sensory environment, waiting, unpredictability, communication or physical accessibility. A visual agenda, preview of the route, step-by-step instructions, pause option, chosen partner or adaptation of material can help — if they correspond to the specific need. Participation does not require everyone to do the activity in the same way.

Prepare activities and outings together

Before a visit, sporting activity or meal, agree with the team the objectives, sequence, available support, communication and relevant safety procedures. Ask the student what helps them and how they prefer to take a break. Record what worked to improve future activities. The solution must promote belonging, dignity and autonomy, preventing absence from being the automatic response to a need for support.

An inclusive school also measures participation in less structured moments: these are opportunities to learn, belong and build relationships.

Frequently asked questions

Does this theme apply the same to all children?

No. The decision must consider development, preferences, context and individual assessment.

When should I ask for help?

When the difficulty persists, affects communication, learning, safety or well-being, talk to qualified professionals and the school, depending on the context.

Does this information replace an individual assessment?

No. This article is general information. Decisions should be based on the child’s needs, context and assessment by qualified professionals.

Where can families find reliable guidance?

Start with the official sources linked in this article and discuss the child’s situation with the school and relevant qualified professionals.

This article is for information only and does not replace an assessment by a health professional. If you are worried about a child’s development, talk to their doctor or paediatrician.
