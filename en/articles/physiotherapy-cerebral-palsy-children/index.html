Physiotherapy for children with cerebral palsy

Physiotherapy may be part of multidisciplinary cerebral palsy care, with goals chosen around a child’s mobility, comfort, health and participation. A plan should not rely on generic exercises or assume every child aims to walk in the same way.

What happens in sessions?

The physiotherapist assesses movement and relevant tasks and may practise motor skills, transfers, balance, mobility or equipment use, depending on the plan. Intervention should be coordinated with the child, family and team; pain, fatigue, posture and environmental access also matter. NICE recommends an integrated team including physiotherapy when appropriate.

How are goals chosen and reviewed?

A goal may be joining play, moving around school, changing position comfortably or accessing a valued activity. The team records what the child can do, with what support and in which setting, then reviews the plan when health or routines change. Ask whether the activity is safe, enjoyable and useful to the child; complementary therapies should not delay indicated medical care.

In short

  • Goals depend on individual needs, preferences and assessment.
  • The team should explain the plan and review its effects on participation and wellbeing.

For information only. It does not replace individual assessment or clinical advice.

Frequently asked questions

Does physiotherapy cure cerebral palsy?

No. It may support function and participation but does not remove the condition.

Must a child practise walking?

No. Goals should reflect the child’s priorities, abilities, health and choices.

This article is for information only and does not replace an assessment by a health professional. If you are worried about a child’s development, talk to their doctor or paediatrician.
