Hearing tests for children: when to ask and which tests exist

Good hearing is the gateway to speech. So whenever there is a concern about language, one of the first questions professionals ask is: “Has hearing been checked?” Even if the baby passed the newborn screen.

By the Talk+ team 25 October 2026 3 min read
Loading reads…

Asking for a hearing test for a child is a simple, painless step that helps rule out, or find early, an important cause of speech delay. In this article we explain which hearing tests exist at each age, what they show and why a normal newborn screen does not rule out a new assessment.

In short

  • Any speech delay should include a hearing assessment.
  • Passing the newborn screen does not rule out hearing loss that appears or progresses later.
  • There are tests suited to every age, from newborns to school age.
  • The earlier hearing loss is identified, the better the outlook for language development.

When to ask for a hearing assessment

  • There is a speech delay or speech is hard to understand for the child’s age.
  • The child does not react to sounds, does not turn when called or often asks for repetition.
  • They turn the TV up very loud or move close to sound sources.
  • They have frequent ear infections or seem to have “blocked” ears (see Glue ear and speech).
  • There has been meningitis, a neonatal unit stay, cytomegalovirus infection or a family history of childhood deafness.

Which tests exist?

TestUsual ageWhat it checks
Otoacoustic emissions (OAE)From birthThe inner ear’s response; quick and done while the child is calm.
Auditory brainstem response (ABR)From birthThe hearing nerve’s response, without needing the child’s cooperation.
TympanometryAny ageHow the eardrum moves; detects fluid in the middle ear.
Visual reinforcement audiometryAbout 6 months to 2–3 yearsThe child turns to the sound and is “rewarded” with a picture or light.
Play audiometryAbout 2–3 to 5 yearsThe child makes a game move (drops a block) every time they hear a sound.
Pure-tone audiometryFrom about age 5The child raises a hand or presses a button when they hear.

The choice of test depends on the child’s age and cooperation. It is usually requested by the GP or paediatrician and carried out by an audiologist, often together with an ear, nose and throat (ENT) team.

They passed the newborn screen. Is that enough?

Newborn hearing screening is very important, but it is not definitive. Some types of hearing loss are progressive or late-onset, and ear infections can lower hearing temporarily. International guidelines recommend monitoring hearing throughout childhood and reassessing whenever there is a concern about speech (Joint Committee on Infant Hearing, 2019).

Ask for an assessment promptly if

  • the baby does not startle at loud noises or settle to their parents’ voices;
  • the child has stopped responding to sounds they used to respond to;
  • words or speech that were there have been lost.

Frequently asked questions about hearing tests

At what age can a hearing test be done?

At any age. Newborns have OAE or ABR tests; older children have audiometry adapted to their age.

Does a hearing test hurt?

No. The tests are painless; some just need the child to be calm or asleep.

My baby passed the newborn screen. Could they still have hearing loss?

Yes. Some hearing loss appears or worsens later, and ear infections can lower hearing. If in doubt, reassess.

Who requests the test?

Usually the GP or paediatrician, who refers to audiology or ENT.

This article is for information only and does not replace an assessment by a health professional. If you are worried about a child’s development, talk to their doctor or paediatrician.
