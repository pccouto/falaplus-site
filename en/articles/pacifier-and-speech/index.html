Does a pacifier delay speech? What we know

A pacifier soothes many babies, and the question of its effect on speech is an old one. The few studies available point in a cautious direction: what deserves attention is very frequent daytime use, especially after the first year.

By the Talk+ team 24 October 2026 3 min read

Does a pacifier delay speech? In this article we summarise what is known about dummies and speech development, why the evidence is limited and what to do in practice so the pacifier does not take up conversation time.

In short

  • Evidence on pacifiers and speech is scarce and observational: it shows associations, not causes.
  • In a British study, most speech outcomes were not associated with pacifier use.
  • Very frequent daytime use was linked to more atypical speech sound errors.
  • In practice: pacifier for sleep or soothing, out of the mouth when the child plays and talks.

What do studies say about pacifiers and speech?

A study published in 2021 looked at the duration and frequency of pacifier use and the development of speech sounds. Most speech outcomes were not associated with pacifier use, but there was an association between frequent daytime use and more atypical errors in speech sounds (Strutt, Khattab and Willoughby, 2021).

The authors stress that only prolonged use, for several hours during the day, seems to start having any effect, and that the evidence base is very small. As an observational study, it does not prove the pacifier is the cause: children who use it a lot may differ in other ways.

Why can daytime use get in the way?

  • Fewer chances to talkWith a pacifier in, the child babbles and responds less.
  • Tongue positionWhile sucking, the tongue stays forward and low.
  • Other effectsProlonged use is also linked to changes in tooth position and more ear infections, topics for the doctor or dentist.

What we can say

  • Evidence is limited and shows associations.
  • Very frequent daytime use deserves attention.
  • Taking the pacifier out to talk is good practice.

What we cannot say

  • «Any pacifier causes speech delay.»
  • «Taking the pacifier away fixes a speech delay.»
  • «A pacifier at bedtime is a problem for speech.»

When to stop the pacifier and how to cut back

  • Keep the pacifier for sleep and soothing, especially after the first year.
  • Take it out during play and meals, and when the child wants to say something.
  • Offer other ways to soothe: a cuddle, a soft toy, a song.
  • If you are worried about speech, ask for an assessment: the pacifier is rarely the only explanation.

Frequently asked questions about pacifiers and speech

Does a pacifier delay speech?

Evidence is thin. Most speech outcomes were not linked to pacifier use; very frequent daytime use was linked to more sound errors.

When should a child stop using a pacifier?

There is no single age. Good practice is to reduce daytime use after the first year and keep it for sleep.

Is a pacifier bad for teeth?

Prolonged use is linked to changes in tooth position. Talk to the dentist or doctor.

Will taking the pacifier away make my child talk?

Not on its own. A speech delay should be assessed; the pacifier is rarely the only explanation.

This article is for information only and does not replace an assessment by a health professional. If you are worried about a child’s development, talk to their doctor or paediatrician.
