How is developmental language disorder assessed?

Assessment for possible developmental language disorder is not just a word count. It aims to understand receptive and expressive language, functional communication and impact on relationships, participation and learning.

By the Talk+ team 18 October 2026 3 min read
Loading reads…

Which methods may be combined?

A professional may gather history, language samples, interaction observations, caregiver and teacher information, and measures suited to age and question. Hearing and other areas may also be considered when indicated. A standardised test should not be interpreted outside the population for which it was validated.

How should a multilingual child be assessed?

Exposure to two or more languages does not cause a disorder. Skills may be distributed differently across languages and change with experience and context. Where possible, assessment considers all relevant languages and uses family information, samples, observation and suitable measures, avoiding direct comparison with inappropriate monolingual norms.

What should the report explain?

The document should describe strengths and difficulties, functional impact, methods and limitations. If a language could not be assessed directly, that limitation should be clear. Recommendations should support communication and participation, not just present a score.

In short

  • Language assessment combines understanding, expression, samples and everyday functioning.
  • For multilingual children, experience in each language matters; a monolingual score may not be valid.

Conclusion

Ask the report to explain what was assessed in each language and how conclusions guide support. No milestone list or isolated count replaces that analysis.
