Speech delay at 18 months: what to expect and when to worry

At 18 months, some toddlers already say dozens of words and others say two or three. The range is wide, so it is worth looking beyond the word count: gestures, eye contact and understanding matter as much as speech.

By the Talk+ team 25 October 2026 3 min read

If you are worried about a possible speech delay at 18 months, this article helps you understand what is expected at this age, which signs to look for and when it is worth asking for an assessment. For an overview, see Speech delay: warning signs.

In short

  • At 18 months, many children say at least three words besides “mama” and “dada”.
  • Pointing to show, looking at the adult and following simple instructions matter as much as talking.
  • A child with few words but good understanding and lots of gestures generally has a better outlook.
  • No words and no gestures at 18 months, or a loss of skills, means an assessment is needed.

What is expected at 18 months?

According to the milestones revised by the US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention and the American Academy of Pediatrics, by 18 months most children try to say three or more words besides “mama” or “dada”, follow one-step instructions without gestures (“give it to me”) and point to show something interesting (Zubler and colleagues, 2022).

  • WordsAt least three words besides “mama” and “dada”, even if still imperfect.
  • GesturesPoints to ask and to show, waves, shakes head for “no”.
  • UnderstandingRecognises names of familiar people and objects and follows simple requests.
  • InteractionLooks at the adult to share interest and copies actions.

Signs to raise with a doctor

Ask for an assessment if, at 18 months, the child

  • says no meaningful words;
  • does not point to show things or use other gestures;
  • does not seem to understand familiar words or respond to their name;
  • has lost words or skills they had (see Language regression).

The doctor may request a hearing assessment and, if needed, refer to speech and language therapy or a developmental clinic.

What you can do now

  • Talk about what the child is looking at or doing, in short sentences.
  • Wait a few seconds after speaking, giving the child time to respond with a sound, gesture or word.
  • Offer choices: “Do you want the apple or the banana?”, showing both.
  • Share picture books and name what you see (see Dialogic reading).
  • Cut screen time without an adult and increase one-to-one play.

Frequently asked questions about speech delay at 18 months

How many words should an 18-month-old say?

Many say at least three words besides “mama” and “dada”, but there is wide variation. Gestures and understanding count too.

The 18-month-old only points and does not talk. Is it a speech delay?

Pointing is a good sign of communication. If there are no words at all, it is worth talking to a doctor and keeping a close eye.

Should I wait until age 2 to ask for help?

Not if there are warning signs. An early assessment does no harm and lets support start sooner.

Does a speech delay at 18 months mean autism?

Not necessarily. But if there is also little eye contact, little pointing or little response to name, an assessment is important.
