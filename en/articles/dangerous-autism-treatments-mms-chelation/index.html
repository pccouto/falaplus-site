MMS, chelation and other dangerous autism «treatments»

Some products and therapies are sold as a «cure» for autism or a way to «unlock» speech. Some have no proven benefit and several have caused serious harm and deaths.

By the Talk+ team 5 November 2026 3 min read
Loading reads…

This article summarises the documented risks of some alternative autism treatments: MMS (chlorine dioxide), chelation, hyperbaric oxygen and secretin, and gives criteria to spot misleading promises.

In short

  • Autism has no «cure» because it is not a poisoning or an infection.
  • MMS is chlorine dioxide, a bleaching agent; the FDA warns that drinking it can cause severe vomiting, dehydration and acute liver failure.
  • Chelation to «remove metals» has caused deaths of children in the United States.
  • Secretin and hyperbaric oxygen have no proven benefit in autism.

Does MMS (chlorine dioxide) cure autism?

No. «MMS» or «CDS» is chlorine dioxide, a product used for bleaching and disinfecting. It is promoted as a drink or enema for autistic children. The US Food and Drug Administration has warned since 2010, with a renewed alert in 2019, not to drink it, describing severe vomiting, severe diarrhoea, life-threatening dehydration and acute liver failure. What is presented as «expelled parasites» is often damaged gut lining. Health authorities in other countries have issued similar warnings.

Does chelation help autism?

Chelation uses medicines that bind heavy metals to treat real poisoning, such as lead poisoning confirmed by blood tests. The idea that autism is caused by metals has no scientific support. In the United States, between 2003 and 2005, deaths from low blood calcium were reported after chelation, including a 5-year-old autistic child (Brown et al., 2006).

What about hyperbaric oxygen and secretin?

  • Hyperbaric oxygen: the FDA warns that it is not cleared or proven for autism and may lead families to delay effective support; it also warns of risks, including fire.
  • Secretin: the Cochrane review of randomised trials found no benefit for communication or behaviour (Williams et al., 2012).

How can you spot a misleading promise?

  • It promises to «cure» or «recover» autism or to «unlock» speech.
  • It works «for everything» and has no side effects.
  • It relies on testimonials and videos, not published trials.
  • It asks you to hide the treatment from the doctor or stop other support.
  • It requires buying products from the promoter.

Seek urgent medical help if the child was given one of these products and has:

  • severe vomiting or diarrhoea, blood in the stool or signs of dehydration;
  • unusual drowsiness, seizures or difficulty breathing.

What we know

  • MMS, and chelation without proven poisoning, have serious documented risks.
  • Secretin and hyperbaric oxygen have shown no benefit in autism.
  • Support for communication and participation is evidence-based.

Myths

  • «Autism is a poisoning that can be cleaned out.»
  • «If it is natural or alternative, it cannot hurt.»
  • «Worsening symptoms show it is working.»

Frequently asked questions about alternative autism treatments

Is there a cure for autism?

Autism is not an illness to be cured. There is evidence-based support that improves communication, independence and quality of life.

Is MMS or chlorine dioxide dangerous?

Yes. Health authorities warn it can cause severe vomiting, dehydration and liver failure.

Does chelation remove the metals that cause autism?

There is no evidence that autism is caused by metals. Chelation has caused deaths.

How do I know whether a treatment has evidence?

Ask the doctor, look for published trials and systematic reviews, and be wary of promises of a cure.

This article is for information only and does not replace an assessment by a health professional. If you are worried about a child’s development, talk to their doctor or paediatrician.
