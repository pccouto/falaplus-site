Speech delay: how many words should a child say and how to count them

“How many words do they say?” It is one of the first questions at a developmental appointment, and many families are not sure how to answer. Making a list helps show the real picture, track progress and decide when to ask for help.

By the Talk+ team 25 October 2026 3 min read
Loading reads…

Knowing how many words a child should say helps you tell whether there is a speech delay, but the numbers are only guides: the variation between children is huge. In this article we explain what is expected at each age, what counts as a word and how to make a useful list. See also Speech delay.

In short

  • The variation in word count between children of the same age is very large.
  • A widely used guideline: fewer than 50 words or no two-word phrases at 24 months justifies assessment.
  • Forms used consistently with the same meaning count as words, even if imperfect.
  • A list kept over a week gives a truer picture than memory.

How many words at each age?

AgeApproximate guide
12 monthsOne or two words (for example “mama”, “dada”, “water”), plus gestures.
18 monthsAt least three words besides “mama” and “dada”; many children say quite a few more.
24 monthsAbout 50 words or more, and two-word combinations.
3 yearsHundreds of words, hard to count; the focus shifts to sentences and clarity.

A study of thousands of children showed huge variation in vocabulary between children of the same age (Fenson and colleagues, 1994). So professionals use guidelines such as 50 words at 24 months (Rescorla, 1989) together with understanding, gestures and interaction.

What counts as a word?

Counts

  • Imperfect words always used with the same meaning (“wawa” for water).
  • Animal or vehicle sounds used to name things (“woof” for dog).
  • Words said without imitation, on the child’s own initiative.

Usually does not count

  • Sounds with no clear meaning.
  • A word said only once.
  • Words the child only repeats straight after the adult.

Gestures and signs are also very important, but it helps to record them on a separate list.

How to make the word list

  1. For a week, keep a sheet or a phone note always to hand.
  2. Write down each word the child uses, how they say it and what it means.
  3. Group by topic: people, food, animals, toys, actions, “more”, “no”, “all gone”.
  4. Ask other adults (grandparents, nursery staff) to add the ones they hear.
  5. Also note gestures and two-word combinations.
  6. Repeat the list every month or two to see progress.

Take the list to the appointment: it helps the doctor and speech and language therapist a lot (see Speech delay treatment).

Frequently asked questions about how many words a child should say

How many words should a 2-year-old say?

About 50 or more, and starting to join two. Fewer than 50 words at 24 months is a guideline for assessment.

How many words should an 18-month-old say?

At least three besides “mama” and “dada”, but many children say quite a few more.

Do mispronounced words count?

Yes, if they are used consistently with the same meaning.

Do gestures count as words?

They are very important for communication, but it helps to record them separately.

This article is for information only and does not replace an assessment by a health professional. If you are worried about a child’s development, talk to their doctor or paediatrician.
