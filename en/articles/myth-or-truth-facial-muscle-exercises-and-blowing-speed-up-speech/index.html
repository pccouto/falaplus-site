Myth: Blowing, making faces or strengthening the muscles in your mouth are not, in themselves, a proven method for speeding up speech. Producing speech requires specific movements and coordination, and the intervention must practice skills related to the identified difficulty.

Speech is not just muscular strength

Breathing and nonverbal oral movements are different from coordinating tongue, lips, breathing, and voice to produce speech sounds. Scientific reviews have not found sufficient support for nonverbal oromotor exercises as a treatment for speech sound disorders. This does not apply to exercises indicated for medical or dietary reasons after specific assessment.

What to look for in an intervention

Ask the speech therapist to explain the clinical hypothesis, the goal, and how the practice connects to the necessary speech sounds or movements. The intervention must be appropriate to the diagnosis and monitor functional changes. Fun activities can be used for other reasons, but should not be presented as a guarantee of faster speech.

In short

  • Blowing bubbles or making faces has not been shown to speed up speech.
  • The intervention must work on specific and evaluated speech objectives.

In summary

If your child has difficulty speaking, seek an individual assessment rather than relying on generic exercises.

Informative content. It does not replace individual assessment or clinical or educational guidance.

Frequently asked questions

Does this theme apply the same to all children?

No. The decision must consider development, preferences, context and individual assessment.

When should I ask for help?

When the difficulty persists, affects communication, learning, safety or well-being, talk to qualified professionals and the school, depending on the context.

Does this information replace an individual assessment?

No. This article is general information. Decisions should be based on the child’s needs, context and assessment by qualified professionals.

Where can families find reliable guidance?

Start with the official sources linked in this article and discuss the child’s situation with the school and relevant qualified professionals.

This article is for information only and does not replace an assessment by a health professional. If you are worried about a child’s development, talk to their doctor or paediatrician.
