Preparing for a child development appointment does not require a perfect file. A short list of questions, everyday examples and relevant documents is usually more useful than trying to record everything a child does.

What is worth gathering?

Bring two or three sentences explaining the reason for the appointment, relevant health, hearing, medication and developmental information, and previous reports that matter. School or other professionals may offer examples, but you do not need an opinion from everyone. Ask beforehand whether a specific form is required.

Which examples help?

Choose concrete situations: how the child asks for help, understands directions, plays, communicates pain or responds to change. A short video may help when something does not happen in the clinic, provided privacy is protected and the service accepts it. Include strengths and support that already works.

Which questions should you take?

Ask about the purpose of the appointment, what will be observed, what can and cannot be concluded, and the next steps. At the end, clarify who is responsible for each action and when review will happen. Not knowing an answer is not a failure; it can be followed up later.

Frequently asked questions

Must I bring videos?

No. They are only one possible source of context and should be shared only when relevant and accepted by the service.

Should I bring every report?

Bring those that help answer the current question. You can start with a list and ask which documents are needed.

Should the child attend?

It depends on the appointment. Check with the service, particularly if the first discussion is for adults only.

Can I bring written questions?

Yes. A short list helps you remember priorities and confirm next steps.

This article is for information only and does not replace an assessment by a health professional. If you are worried about a child’s development, talk to their doctor or paediatrician.
