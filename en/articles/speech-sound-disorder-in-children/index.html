Speech sound errors: what is typical and when to get help

«Tat» for «cat», «wabbit» for «rabbit»: sound errors are part of the journey for almost every child. The question is how long they are expected and when they deserve attention.

By the Talk+ team 24 October 2026 3 min read
Loading reads…

Speech sound errors are one of the most common reasons families see a speech and language therapist. In this article we explain when children learn speech sounds, what a speech sound disorder is and which signs justify an assessment.

In short

  • In a review covering 27 languages, children produced at least 93% of consonants correctly by age 5.
  • Sounds such as stops and nasals are learned early; trills, fricatives and affricates later.
  • At age 4, about 3.4% of children had a speech sound disorder in an Australian study.
  • Almost half of those children also had language difficulties.

When do children say all their sounds?

A review of 64 studies, with more than 26,000 children and 27 languages, found that most consonants are acquired by about age 5, when children produced at least 93% of consonants correctly (McLeod and Crowe, 2018). The order is similar across languages:

Type of soundEnglish examplesWhen it usually appears
Stops and nasalsp, b, t, d, m, nearlier
Fricativesf, v, s, z, sh, thlater
Liquidsl, ramong the last
Consonant clusters«spoon», «tree»among the last

These ages are research reference points, not exact targets: children and languages vary.

What is a speech sound disorder?

We talk about a disorder when errors are more frequent or longer-lasting than expected for the child’s age, or when they are unusual. In an Australian study of about 1,500 children, 3.4% had a speech sound disorder at age 4; of these, 40.8% also had a language disorder and 20.8% had weaker pre-literacy skills (Eadie et al., 2015).

  • Age-typical errorsCommon simplifications that fade over time, such as «tat» for «cat» at age 3.
  • Persistent errorsSimplifications that continue well beyond the expected age.
  • Atypical errorsUnusual errors at any age, which deserve an earlier assessment.
  • Apraxia of speechDifficulty planning speech movements; see Childhood apraxia of speech.

Ask a speech and language therapist for an assessment if:

  • at age 3, people outside the family understand little of what the child says;
  • at age 4 to 5, the child still makes many errors or unusual errors;
  • the child gets frustrated or avoids talking because they are not understood;
  • there are also language or hearing difficulties.

How to help without constant correction

  • Repeat the word with the right sound, naturally: «Yes, it’s a cat!», without asking them to repeat it.
  • Value what the child meant before how they said it.
  • Play with rhymes, songs and animal sounds.
  • Avoid blowing or facial exercises as a substitute for therapy: see do facial exercises speed up speech?.

Frequently asked questions about speech sound errors

Is it normal for a 3-year-old to mispronounce words?

Yes, some errors are expected at age 3. The concern is when people outside the family understand little, or when errors are unusual.

When should a child say the «r» sound?

The «r» is among the last sounds acquired. If it still does not appear by age 5 to 6, an assessment is worthwhile.

What is the difference between articulation and phonological errors?

Articulation errors involve producing a specific sound; phonological errors follow patterns that affect groups of sounds. A therapist can tell which is which.

Should I correct my child when they say a sound wrong?

It is better to repeat the word with the right sound, naturally, without asking the child to repeat it.

This article is for information only and does not replace an assessment by a health professional. If you are worried about a child’s development, talk to their doctor or paediatrician.
