An adapted keyboard, communication system, or other assistive technology can remove a concrete barrier to participation. The choice must start from the activity the child wants to carry out and be accompanied by training and adaptation to the context. In Portugal, the Information and Communication Technologies Resource Centers for Special Education (CRTIC) support this process.

What do CRTICs do?

The CRTIC network, coordinated by the Directorate-General for Education, collaborates in assessing the needs for learning support products, providing information on solutions and training professionals and families. The objective is to identify whether and how a technology can facilitate access to the curriculum, communication, autonomy or participation — not to recommend the same equipment for everyone.

How to start the process and monitor usage

Start by describing to the school what task is difficult and what has already been attempted. The school can coordinate with the CRTIC in the area to assess the situation; confirm with the group the applicable documents and steps. The evaluation does not mean automatic approval of financing or any equipment. If recommended, plan configuration, training, maintenance and use in various contexts. Review whether the solution is helping the child to participate.

In summary

A technology is only useful when it works on a routine basis: the child can access it, the people around them know how to support it and its effect is monitored.

Informative content. It does not replace individual assessment or clinical or educational guidance.

Frequently asked questions

Does this theme apply the same to all children?

No. The decision must consider development, preferences, context and individual assessment.

When should I ask for help?

When the difficulty persists, affects communication, learning, safety or well-being, talk to qualified professionals and the school, depending on the context.

Does this information replace an individual assessment?

No. This article is general information. Decisions should be based on the child’s needs, context and assessment by qualified professionals.

Where can families find reliable guidance?

Start with the official sources linked in this article and discuss the child’s situation with the school and relevant qualified professionals.

This article is for information only and does not replace an assessment by a health professional. If you are worried about a child’s development, talk to their doctor or paediatrician.
