PECS: Picture Exchange Communication System, phases and evidence

A child hands an adult a card with a picture of biscuits and gets a biscuit. This simple gesture is the starting point of PECS, one of the best-known picture communication systems in the world.

By the Talk+ team 25 October 2026 3 min read

PECS (the Picture Exchange Communication System) was developed in the United States in the 1980s for autistic children with little or no speech. In this article we explain the PECS phases, what studies say and how the system fits with other forms of augmentative and alternative communication.

In short

  • In PECS, the child learns to start communication by handing a picture to another person.
  • Teaching follows six phases, from a simple request to commenting.
  • Studies show gains in communication; effects on speech are less clear, but PECS does not stop speech.
  • It is one of several AAC options; the choice should start from the child’s needs.

The six phases of PECS

PhaseWhat the child learns
I. How to communicateExchange a picture for something they really want, with physical help from a second adult.
II. Distance and persistenceFetch the card and take it to someone further away, in different places.
III. DiscriminationChoose, from two or more pictures, the one that matches what they want.
IV. Sentence structureBuild “I want” + picture on a sentence strip.
V. Answering questionsAnswer “What do you want?”.
VI. CommentingUse pictures to comment: “I see”, “I hear”.

A key feature of PECS is that the child initiates communication: they approach someone, rather than only responding (Bondy and Frost, 1994).

What do studies say?

A meta-analysis concluded that PECS is promising for increasing communication in autistic children, with weaker and more variable effects on speech, and that more research is needed on maintenance and generalisation of gains (Flippin, Reszka and Watson, 2010). Other reviews of picture- or device-based AAC reach similar conclusions (Ganz and colleagues, 2012).

What can be said

  • PECS can help a child request and start communicating.
  • Using pictures does not stop speech from developing.
  • It works best when used across settings and by several people.

What cannot be said

  • “PECS always makes a child start talking.”
  • “You can only move to a tablet after finishing PECS.”
  • “PECS suits every child without speech.”

PECS, tablets and other forms of AAC

PECS is one of several options. Some children start with cards and move on to a speech-generating device; others use several systems at once. The choice should be made with a speech and language therapist, considering the child’s motor skills, vision, understanding and preferences (see How to choose a communication app and Does AAC stop speech?).

Frequently asked questions about PECS

What is PECS?

A picture exchange communication system in which the child hands a card to another person to request or comment.

Does PECS help children talk?

Studies show clearer effects on communication than on speech. It does not stop speech and, in some children, may go alongside it.

At what age can PECS start?

There is no fixed minimum age. It is used mostly with young children, but also with older children and adults.

Can I make PECS cards at home?

You can use pictures at home, but teaching the phases follows a specific protocol. Ask a speech and language therapist for guidance.
