When should you seek a developmental assessment?

You do not need to wait for a specific age or a complete checklist of signs to discuss development. A persistent concern from family or people who know the child should be shared with a health professional.

By the Talk+ team 18 October 2026 3 min read
Loading reads…

What situations are useful to describe?

Give examples of what the child understands, communicates, learns, does independently and how they participate in different routines. Say when it began, whether it occurs in different settings and what helps or makes it harder. A missed milestone is a reason to discuss it, not a diagnosis on its own.

Why act before knowing a diagnosis?

Screening and assessment can clarify needs, but functional support can begin while assessment is under way. Developmental surveillance guidance recommends responding to concerns and referring when indicated rather than waiting passively for an age or score.

What if skills are lost?

Loss of words or other previously acquired skills should be shared promptly with a health professional. Note when it was noticed and what changed, without trying to determine the cause at home. If the change is sudden or occurs with other worrying symptoms, seek urgent clinical care.

In short

  • Share persistent concerns early; a diagnosis or completed checklist is not required.
  • Loss of acquired skills warrants prompt clinical contact.

Conclusion

Concrete examples help a professional decide which assessment or support may be appropriate. Seeking guidance can start before there is a name for the difficulty.
