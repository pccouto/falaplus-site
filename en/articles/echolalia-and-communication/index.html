Echolalia: why does a child repeat words?

Echolalia is the repetition of words, phrases or sounds a child has heard. It may happen soon after hearing them (immediate echolalia) or later (delayed echolalia), such as repeating a line from a story. To understand what it means in a particular moment, consider the context instead of assuming the repetition has no communicative purpose.

Is echolalia always a sign of autism?

No. Echolalia is often described in the communication of some autistic people, but repetition alone cannot establish that a child is autistic. Assessment considers development, communication across settings and the individual profile. If there are broader concerns, talk with a professional.

How should I respond when a child repeats phrases?

  • Notice what happened before and after: the child may be answering, requesting, commenting or joining a routine.
  • Respond to the possible meaning and naturally add words.
  • Allow processing time and accept different communication modes, including gestures and pictures.
  • Avoid demanding an explanation or forbidding familiar repeated phrases.
  • Share examples with the child’s support team to help understand patterns and needs.

The meaning of repetition depends on the child and situation; there is no single interpretation that applies to everyone.

Frequently asked questions

What is immediate echolalia?

Repeating something heard shortly beforehand, such as a word or phrase. Its function needs to be understood in context.

What is delayed echolalia?

Repeating words or phrases after some time, sometimes from songs, videos or earlier conversations.

Does echolalia in autism mean a child does not understand?

Understanding cannot be inferred from repetition alone. The child’s communication needs to be considered individually and broadly.

Should I correct a child who repeats words?

Instead of correcting or interrupting, try responding to what they may be communicating and offer language models without pressure.
