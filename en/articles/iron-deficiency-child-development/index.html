Iron deficiency in children: anaemia, development and supplements

Iron deficiency is the most common nutritional deficiency in the world. In babies and toddlers it is linked to developmental delays. But too much iron is dangerous, so supplements depend on blood tests.

By the Talk+ team 5 November 2026 3 min read
Loading reads…

Iron deficiency in children is a real concern: iron is needed for brain development in the early years. Here we explain what studies show about anaemia and child development and how to prevent it safely.

In short

  • Iron-deficiency anaemia in babies is linked to poorer cognitive, motor and social-emotional development, and some effects may last.
  • These are associations: poverty and other factors also play a part, and iron is not a treatment for speech delay in children without a deficiency.
  • Prevention relies on a suitable diet and, in at-risk groups, on supplements prescribed by a doctor.
  • Too much iron is toxic: keep iron-containing medicines out of children's reach.

Can iron deficiency delay development?

Studies that followed babies with iron-deficiency anaemia found lower scores on cognitive, motor and behavioural tests, and some followed these children for years (Lozoff et al., 2006). The American Academy of Pediatrics considers that iron deficiency in the early years may have neurodevelopmental effects that do not always reverse with treatment (Baker and Greer, 2010). That is why prevention matters.

Keep two points in mind: these studies are mostly observational (association is not causation), and giving iron to a child without a deficiency does not improve speech.

Which children are at higher risk?

  • Babies born premature or with low birth weight.
  • Babies given cow's milk as a main drink before 12 months, or large amounts afterwards.
  • Children with very selective or restricted eating, common in some autistic children.
  • Children with blood loss or conditions that affect absorption.

How can it be prevented?

From the start of solid foods, iron-rich foods (meat, fish, eggs, pulses, fortified cereals), with vitamin C in the same meal. In at-risk groups, the doctor may prescribe a supplement at a set dose and duration. Screening and supplement recommendations follow each country's health guidance.

Iron is dangerous in excess:

  • store iron supplements and vitamins containing iron out of children's reach: accidental iron poisoning is a known cause of serious poisoning in young children;
  • do not give iron «just in case» without a blood test;
  • if the child has swallowed iron tablets, call emergency services or your local poison centre straight away.

What we know

  • Iron deficiency in babies is linked to developmental delays.
  • Prevention matters, especially in risk groups.
  • Diagnosis is made with blood tests.

What cannot be said

  • «Iron makes a child start talking.»
  • «An iron supplement cannot hurt anyone.»
  • «A pale child definitely has anaemia.»

Frequently asked questions about iron and anaemia

Can anaemia cause speech delay?

Iron-deficiency anaemia in babies is linked to developmental delays, but it is rarely the only explanation for speech delay. Both should be assessed.

Can I give my child iron without a prescription?

It is not recommended. Too much iron is toxic, and a deficiency is diagnosed with blood tests.

What are the signs of low iron in a child?

There may be paleness, tiredness, irritability or poor appetite, but many children have no signs. Only blood tests confirm it.

Which foods are high in iron?

Meat, fish, eggs, pulses and fortified cereals. Vitamin C in the same meal helps absorb iron from plant foods.

This article is for information only and does not replace an assessment by a health professional. If you are worried about a child’s development, talk to their doctor or paediatrician.
