How many therapy sessions does a child need?

There is no universal number of therapy sessions for a child. Dosage includes frequency, duration and intensity, and should be justified by the goal, approach, progress and effect of the plan on daily life.

What determines frequency?

The difficulty, evidence for the chosen approach, age, goal, practice needs, health and the child’s tolerance may affect the plan. Direct sessions, family coaching, groups, consultation and routine-based practice are different models; counting visits alone does not describe the whole intervention.

Do more sessions mean a better outcome?

Not necessarily. Suitable intensity varies, and too much may cause fatigue, reduce time for play, school and family, or make the plan unmanageable. Lower frequency also needs a rationale and monitoring. The key question is whether the model offers enough opportunity for the agreed goal without overload.

When should the plan be reviewed?

The team should agree from the start when progress, participation, wellbeing and barriers will be reviewed. If the child changes, a goal is met, expected progress is absent or routines become unsustainable, frequency and delivery can be reconsidered. Ask for the reasoning to be clear.

Frequently asked questions

Is one session a week enough?

It may or may not be; this depends on goals, delivery model and progress monitoring.

Must therapy continue into adulthood?

A diagnosis alone does not decide this. Support is reviewed according to needs, goals and benefit.

Will a break undo everything?

There is no universal answer. Plan the break and review with the team.

Does school support count as therapy?

School support and therapy may have different roles; goals should be coordinated without confusing services.
