Augmentative and alternative communication (AAC): what it is and how it helps

Not every child communicates mainly through speech. Some use gestures, signs, pictures or a tablet that speaks for them. Find out what AAC is, who it is for and why it does not stop a child from talking.

By the Talk+ team 14 October 2026 4 min read

Augmentative and alternative communication (AAC) covers the ways of communicating that add to (augmentative) or replace (alternative) spoken language. In this article we explain how it works, who can benefit and how to bring it into everyday life.

In short

  • AAC includes gestures, signs, picture boards and apps with voice output.
  • It can help anyone whose speech is not enough for everything they need to say.
  • The available research does not suggest that AAC stops speech.
  • There are no prerequisites: nobody needs to «be ready» to start.

What is AAC? Examples

It is any form of communication beyond speech. It falls into two broad groups:

  • UnaidedGestures, facial expressions, signs and sign language.
  • AidedPicture or symbol boards and books, cards, letters, communication boards and apps with voice output.

Most people use AAC in a multimodal way: they speak when they can, point when it is quicker and use a picture when the word will not come.

Who is AAC for?

It can help children and adults who, for any reason, cannot communicate everything they need through speech. For example:

  • autistic children with little spoken language;
  • children with childhood apraxia of speech or dysarthria;
  • cerebral palsy and other motor conditions;
  • some genetic syndromes, such as Down syndrome;
  • language delays, while speech is developing;
  • temporary situations, such as recovery after surgery.

«If they use pictures, they’ll stop trying to talk»?

It is the most common worry, and research does not support it. A 2025 systematic review of autistic children with minimal speech suggests that combining AAC with naturalistic interventions may benefit language. The studies vary and the evidence has limits, but there is no indication that AAC stops speech.

It makes sense: when a child has a way to be understood, they communicate more, feel less frustrated and hear the word linked to the picture many times.

«They have to show they’re ready»?

AAC has no prerequisites of age, cognition, speech or reading. Nobody has to «earn» a way to communicate. Professional guidance recommends starting early rather than waiting for a diagnosis.

How to bring AAC into everyday life

  1. Model. The adult also taps the pictures while talking («Would you like water?» and taps «water»). Children learn AAC the way they learn speech: by seeing others use it.
  2. Start with motivating requests: favourite food, toys, «more», «finished».
  3. Keep AAC within reach all the time, not only during «practice».
  4. Accept every form of response: picture, gesture, sound or word.
  5. Go beyond requests: commenting («I like it», «funny»), refusing («I don’t want it»), greeting and sharing feelings.
  6. Involve everyone around the child. Family, school and therapists should use the same vocabulary.

Choosing the right AAC system for each child should be done with a speech and language therapist.

Frequently asked questions about augmentative and alternative communication

Is AAC only for autistic children?

No. It is useful for anyone whose speech does not meet all their communication needs, temporarily or permanently.

What are some examples of AAC?

Unaided: gestures, facial expressions, signs and sign language. Aided: communication boards, picture cards and books, letter boards and devices or apps that speak messages aloud. Many people combine several forms.

Can a communication board help autistic children?

It can help many autistic children with little spoken language to ask, choose and share, and research does not suggest that AAC stops speech. Every child is different, so a speech and language therapist helps choose the system and vocabulary.

Does AAC replace speech therapy?

No. AAC is a way of communicating; speech and language therapy assesses, chooses and teaches the right system, among other goals.

How old does a child need to be to use AAC?

There is no minimum age. Babies and young children can also benefit from gestures and pictures.

Does a paper communication board work?

Yes. Paper and apps have different advantages: paper never runs out of battery; an app gives messages a voice and allows much more vocabulary.

This article is for information only and does not replace an assessment by a health professional. If you are worried about a child’s development, talk to their doctor or paediatrician.
