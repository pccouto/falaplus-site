Myth or fact: does an app report assess development?

“Can a game report confirm a developmental delay?” No. An automated record may summarise responses in an app, but it was not designed to diagnose a condition or assess a child comprehensively.

By the Talk+ team 18 October 2026 3 min read
Loading reads…

What does a game report measure?

It measures only variables defined for that app and collected during use, such as responses, words explored and activity time. Results depend on the game, familiarity, attention at that moment, hints and which data were recorded.

A clinical assessment tool needs a defined purpose, method and population, supported by evidence of validity and reliability. An in-app game metric does not become a clinical test because it appears in a PDF or chart.

Why is it not a diagnosis?

Diagnosis requires relevant information to be considered against clinical criteria, including developmental history, functioning, context and possible alternative explanations. For autism, for example, no single tool should form the basis of diagnosis; caregiver accounts, professional observation and clinical judgement are combined.

A game report does not observe communication at home and school, gather a full clinical history or check diagnostic criteria. It therefore cannot confirm or rule out a condition.

What should you do with the data?

If there is a concern, bring everyday examples to a health professional and ask whether the report helps with the specific question. Assessment should follow the needs and concerns observed, not depend on an app score.

Communication and participation support can be discussed while assessment is under way. A diagnostic label is not a prerequisite for asking for guidance or responding to a functional need.

In short

  • Myth: a game report cannot confirm or rule out a diagnosis.
  • It describes only what the app recorded and does not replace clinical assessment or everyday observation.

Conclusion

Read any digital report in light of what was actually measured. If you are concerned about development, speak with a qualified professional instead of treating a score as a diagnostic answer.
