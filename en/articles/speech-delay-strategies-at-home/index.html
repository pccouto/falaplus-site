Speech delay: 8 strategies to use at home every day

You do not need special materials or free hours. The strategies speech therapists teach families fit into bath time, car journeys and snacks. The secret lies in small changes in how you talk, repeated many times a day.

By the Talk+ team 25 October 2026 3 min read
Loading reads…

If you are looking for what to do at home about speech delay, these eight strategies are a good starting point. They come from programmes where parents learn to boost language in daily life, which show good results in young children. They do not replace an assessment: see Speech delay.

In short

  • Small changes in how you talk, repeated in routines, make a difference.
  • Waiting and giving time is one of the simplest and most forgotten strategies.
  • Responding to what the child says and adding a word helps them take the next step.
  • Fewer questions and more comments make conversation easier.

The 8 strategies

  1. Get down to the child’s level, face to face, so they can see your face and mouth.
  2. Follow the child’s interest: talk about what they are looking at or doing, not what you want to teach.
  3. Wait: after speaking or showing something, count to five in your head and give time to respond.
  4. Talk alongside: describe what the child is doing (“You’re filling the bucket!”) and what you are doing (“I’m cutting the apple”).
  5. Expand: if they say “car”, reply “Red car!”; if they say “red car”, reply “The red car is going fast!”.
  6. Offer choices: “Milk or juice?”, showing both and saying the words.
  7. Create reasons to communicate: put a favourite toy in sight but out of reach, or give just a little biscuit.
  8. Repeat and use routines: the same words at the same moments (bath, meals, bedtime) help the child anticipate.

What studies say

A meta-analysis of programmes in which parents learned strategies like these showed positive effects on language in young children with delays (Roberts and Kaiser, 2011). In another study, coached parents increased back-and-forth turns with their babies, who had more words at 18 months (Ramírez, Lytle and Kuhl, 2020).

What to avoid

Helps

  • Commenting more than questioning.
  • Accepting gestures and sounds as communication.
  • Modelling the right word without asking for repetition.

Usually gets in the way

  • Constantly saying “Say… say…”.
  • Asking lots of questions in a row.
  • Anticipating everything before the child asks.
  • Background screens during play.

Frequently asked questions about speech delay strategies

What can I do at home to help the child talk?

Get to their level, follow their interest, wait, expand what they say, offer choices and repeat the same words in routines.

How much time a day should I spend?

No special time is needed: the most useful thing is to use the strategies in routines, several times a day.

Should I ask the child to repeat words?

It is not necessary. Model the right word in your reply, naturally.

Do these strategies replace speech therapy?

No. They complement it. If there are warning signs, ask for an assessment.

This article is for information only and does not replace an assessment by a health professional. If you are worried about a child’s development, talk to their doctor or paediatrician.
