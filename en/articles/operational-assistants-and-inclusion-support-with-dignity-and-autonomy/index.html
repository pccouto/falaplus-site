Operational assistants and inclusion: support with dignity and autonomy

Operational assistants are present at many decisive moments in school life. Your collaboration can make routines more predictable and accessible, from arrival to recess. To work well, support needs clear objectives, coordination with the team and respect for the child's autonomy.

Operational assistants are present at many decisive moments in school life. Your collaboration can make routines more predictable and accessible, from arrival to recess. To work well, support needs clear objectives, coordination with the team and respect for the child's autonomy.

A collaborative role, with clear boundaries

Decree-Law No. 54/2018 provides for the participation of school professionals, including operational assistants, in the multidisciplinary team to support inclusive education in certain situations. The form of integration and the concrete tasks depend on the context and assigned functions. Daily support does not replace the pedagogical responsibility of teachers nor the evaluation or intervention of specialized professionals.

Promote participation instead of doing it for the child

When appropriate to the school's roles and direction, the assistant can support transitions, communicate relevant signals to the team, and help maintain agreed strategies. Asking before helping, giving the child time to respond, offering only necessary support and protecting privacy promotes autonomy. Training, passing information between shifts and defined channels for communicating concerns prevent support from relying solely on memory or individual initiative.

In short

  • Inclusion is teamwork and may involve operational assistants.
  • Support means creating conditions for participation, not replacing the child or other professionals.

In summary

Recognizing the contribution of operational assistants also implies ensuring guidance, appropriate training for the tasks and functional communication with the educational team.

Informative content. It does not replace individual assessment or clinical or educational guidance.

Frequently asked questions

Does this theme apply the same to all children?

No. The decision must consider development, preferences, context and individual assessment.

When should I ask for help?

When the difficulty persists, affects communication, learning, safety or well-being, talk to qualified professionals and the school, depending on the context.

Does this information replace an individual assessment?

No. This article is general information. Decisions should be based on the child’s needs, context and assessment by qualified professionals.

Where can families find reliable guidance?

Start with the official sources linked in this article and discuss the child’s situation with the school and relevant qualified professionals.

This article is for information only and does not replace an assessment by a health professional. If you are worried about a child’s development, talk to their doctor or paediatrician.
