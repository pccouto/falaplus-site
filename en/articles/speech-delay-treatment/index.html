Speech delay: treatment and what a speech therapist does

When there is a speech delay, speech and language therapy is the main response. But “therapy” can mean different things: sessions with the child, coaching for the family, work with nursery or school. Knowing what to expect helps you take part and follow progress.

By the Talk+ team 25 October 2026 3 min read
Loading reads…

Speech delay treatment is delivered mainly by a speech and language therapist, starting from an assessment. In this article we explain what happens in therapy, what the evidence says, how long it may last and how to know if it is working. See also The first speech therapy appointment.

In short

  • Treatment starts with an assessment of communication, understanding, speech and hearing.
  • Speech and language therapy has evidence of effectiveness, especially for vocabulary and speech sounds.
  • Programmes in which parents learn everyday strategies show good results in young children.
  • Duration varies; progress is measured against concrete goals reviewed regularly.

How does speech therapy work?

  • AssessmentObserving play, age-appropriate tests, talking with the family and requesting a hearing assessment if not done yet.
  • GoalsConcrete targets, such as “asks with two words at home and at nursery”.
  • SessionsUsually through play, with activities chosen for the goals.
  • Family and schoolStrategies to use at home and in class, because most practice happens outside the session.

What does the evidence say?

A meta-analysis concluded that speech and language therapy is effective for difficulties with expressive vocabulary and speech sounds, with more limited evidence for comprehension, and that intervention delivered by trained parents can be as effective as therapist-only intervention (Law, Garrett and Nye, 2004).

Another meta-analysis showed that programmes where parents learn to use language strategies in daily life have positive effects on young children’s expressive and receptive language (Roberts and Kaiser, 2011). See also Caregiver training.

How long does it last?

There is no fixed duration. It depends on age, type of difficulty, how often sessions happen and practice at home. Some children need a few months; others need support over years, with more and less intensive periods. What matters is reviewing goals regularly.

How to tell if it is working

  • Ask what the current goals are and how they will be measured.
  • Keep a list of new words or phrases each month (see How many words should a child say).
  • Notice whether the child uses what they learned in other places, not just in sessions.
  • Ask for a review meeting every few months.
  • See also How to track small progress.

Frequently asked questions about speech delay treatment

How is speech delay treated?

With speech and language therapy, based on an assessment, with concrete goals and with the family and school taking part.

Does speech therapy work?

Yes, there is evidence of effectiveness, especially for vocabulary and speech sounds. Results vary from child to child.

How many sessions are needed?

It depends on the child. Frequency and duration are set by the therapist and reviewed over time.

Can I help at home?

Yes, and it matters a lot. Ask the therapist for concrete strategies to use in daily routines.

This article is for information only and does not replace an assessment by a health professional. If you are worried about a child’s development, talk to their doctor or paediatrician.
