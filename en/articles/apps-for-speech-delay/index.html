Apps for speech delay: how to choose and use them with a child

There are hundreds of apps promising to “get your child talking”. Some can be a good complement; many are just entertainment. Knowing what to look for, and how to use it, makes the difference between a passive screen and a moment of communication.

By the Talk+ team 25 October 2026 3 min read
Loading reads…

An app for speech delay does not replace conversation or speech and language therapy, but it can be useful if well chosen and used with an adult. In this article we summarise what research says about educational apps, how to choose and good practice. See also Speech delay and Screen time.

In short

  • No app on its own solves a speech delay.
  • The most useful apps ask for active participation, are meaningful to the child and invite interaction with an adult.
  • In the early years, screen time should be short, high-quality and shared.
  • An app can complement speech therapy between sessions.

What makes an app useful for language?

A review of educational apps proposed four criteria: the child should be active (doing, not just watching), engaged (without needless distractions), learning something meaningful linked to their life and in social interaction with other people (Hirsh-Pasek and colleagues, 2015).

Look forBe wary of
Everyday words with picture and clear voicePromises to “get your child talking in days”
The adult can choose the vocabularyConstant ads and purchases
Short activities matched to the child’s levelLots of stimuli, sounds and rewards with no pauses
Room to comment and chatVideos that autoplay with no participation
Clear privacy informationPoorly explained data collection

Screens and age: what paediatricians recommend

The American Academy of Pediatrics recommends avoiding digital media before 18–24 months, except video calls, and, between 2 and 5 years, limiting it to about one hour a day of high-quality content, ideally used with an adult (AAP, 2016). See Do screens cause speech delay?

How to use an app with a child

  • Use it alongside the child: comment, ask, wait for a response.
  • Take the app’s words into real life: if “apple” appeared, find an apple in the kitchen.
  • Choose the vocabulary with the speech and language therapist, in line with the goals.
  • Short sessions, at a calm moment, followed by screen-free play.
  • For children who use AAC, see How to choose a communication app.

Frequently asked questions about apps for speech delay

Can an app help with speech delay?

It can be a useful complement, especially used with an adult and with chosen vocabulary. It does not replace conversation or therapy.

What is the best app to help a child talk?

It depends on the child. Look for active apps with no ads, everyday words and vocabulary the adult can choose.

From what age can an app be used?

Paediatricians recommend avoiding screens before 18–24 months, except video calls, and using them sparingly and together up to age 5.

Can the speech therapist use the same app?

Yes. Using the same app at home and in therapy helps keep goals consistent.
