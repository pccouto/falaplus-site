Myth or fact: does a positive autism screen mean autism?

Myth: a positive screen confirms autism. Screening identifies risk or a possible need for further assessment. A conclusion depends on diagnostic assessment of development, context and clinical criteria.

By the Talk+ team 18 October 2026 3 min read
Loading reads…

What does “positive” mean?

It means responses crossed the threshold defined for that tool and that next steps should be considered. It does not mean a child has a condition. Thresholds, sensitivity and specificity vary across tools and populations.

Why can false positives happen?

A brief tool may flag signs shared by different conditions, developmental variation or assessment circumstances. This does not mean the concern is false; it means screening cannot distinguish every explanation and needs follow-up.

What happens next?

Ask the professional to explain the tool, result and referral. Assessment may gather caregiver information and observe the child; other areas, such as hearing or language, may be considered when indicated. Functional support need not wait for a diagnostic name.

In short

  • A positive screen signals that further assessment should be considered.
  • It does not confirm autism or replace clinical consideration of other explanations.

Conclusion

A positive result can cause anxiety. Ask for a clear explanation and next steps; do not turn a screening threshold into a conclusion about the child.
