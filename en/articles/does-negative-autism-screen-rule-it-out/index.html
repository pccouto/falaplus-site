Myth or fact: does a negative autism screen rule it out?

Myth: a negative screen proves there is no autism. A result below the threshold only means the tool did not flag risk in that administration. It does not replace developmental monitoring or assessment when concerns persist.

By the Talk+ team 18 October 2026 3 min read
Loading reads…

Why might screening not flag a concern?

No tool identifies every case. Age, how questions are understood, language, behaviour that day and tool characteristics may affect results. Sensitivity is not 100%, so false negatives are possible.

What if the concern continues?

Share what you notice with the child’s doctor or professional even after a negative screen. Describe specific situations, frequency, context and changes over time. Clinical guidance says caregiver concerns should be taken seriously.

Should you wait for another screen?

Do not delay a discussion or referral if signs persist, impact is significant or previously acquired skills are lost. The professional can decide whether to repeat screening, gather other information or refer for assessment. Seek clinical guidance promptly if regression occurs.

In short

  • A negative screen does not rule out autism when concerns persist.
  • Talk to a professional; a negative result should not delay indicated assessment.

Conclusion

Use screening as one piece of information, never as permission to ignore concerns. The child’s history and observations from people who know them matter too.
