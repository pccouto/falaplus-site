F80.9, 6A01: what speech codes in a report mean

In a clinical report or a request for support, a code like F80.9 can look like a mysterious label. It is simply the standard way to classify the difficulty identified, and it follows rules worth knowing.

By the Talk+ team 24 October 2026 3 min read

Speech and language diagnosis codes come from the World Health Organization’s International Classification of Diseases (ICD). In this article we explain what the most common ICD-10 (F80) and ICD-11 (6A01) codes mean and what to ask when they appear in a report.

In short

  • ICD-10 groups specific developmental disorders of speech and language under F80; ICD-11 uses block 6A01.
  • F80.9 means «unspecified»: there is a difficulty, but the report does not classify it into a subtype.
  • A code summarises; it does not replace a description of the difficulties and recommended support.
  • Codes can change with new assessments as the child grows.

What are ICD-10 and ICD-11?

The ICD is the World Health Organization classification used worldwide to record diagnoses. ICD-11 came into effect in 2022, but many services still use ICD-10 or national versions of it (such as ICD-10-CM in the United States). So the same difficulty may appear with different codes in different reports.

Which codes are most common for speech and language?

6A01.1F98.5 (approximate)Developmental speech fluency disorder (stuttering)
6A01.20F80.2Language disorder with impaired receptive and expressive language

Matches between ICD-10 and ICD-11 are not always exact. Reports may also refer to the DSM-5 or describe the difficulty without a code. See Speech sound errors in children and Speech delay and DLD.

F80.9: what does «unspecified» mean?

The F80.9 code is used when there is a developmental disorder of speech or language, but the report does not classify it into a subtype. This may happen at a first assessment, with a young child or when information is still incomplete. It does not indicate severity.

The code tells you

  • What kind of difficulty was identified.
  • A shared language between health and education services.

The code does not tell you

  • Severity or prognosis.
  • What support the child needs.
  • That the difficulty is permanent.

What to ask when you receive a report with codes

  1. Which concrete difficulties were observed, and in which situations?
  2. Which tests and observations support the code?
  3. Which goals and support are recommended, and how often?
  4. When should there be a reassessment?

Frequently asked questions about diagnosis codes

What does F80.9 mean in a report?

Developmental disorder of speech or language, unspecified: a difficulty has been identified without classifying it into a subtype. It does not indicate severity.

What is the difference between F80.1 and F80.2?

In ICD-10, F80.1 refers mainly to expressive language and F80.2 to difficulties with both understanding and expression.

What is code 6A01?

It is the ICD-11 block for developmental speech or language disorders.

Can the code change?

Yes. With new assessments and as the child grows, the classification may be updated.

This article is for information only and does not replace an assessment by a health professional. If you are worried about a child’s development, talk to their doctor or paediatrician.
