Should parents attend their child’s therapy sessions?

There is no single rule about parents attending therapy sessions. Decisions should consider age, privacy, goals, the child’s preferences, the intervention model and how the family will receive information and coaching.

When can attendance help?

With young children and routine-based interventions, observing and practising strategies may support use in daily life. Presence may also provide security or communication support. The adult should not turn the session into constant testing or answer for the child every time.

When might some time without parents make sense?

An older child may want privacy, and the clinician may need to observe interaction or independence differently. This should be explained, age-appropriate and compatible with safety and consent. “Parents never come in” is not a substitute for a clear rationale.

How can families stay involved?

Options include a shared beginning or ending, occasional observation, coaching sessions, written goals and routine examples. The child should know what will be shared in an age-appropriate way. Ask how questions can be raised between reviews without invading therapeutic space.

Frequently asked questions

Can I ask to observe a session?

You can ask and discuss how observation fits the plan and privacy needs.

Does not attending mean not participating?

No. Coaching, goal reviews and everyday examples are also forms of participation.

Can the child choose?

Their views should be heard in an age- and communication-appropriate way, within safety and care requirements.

Must the clinician share everything?

Information needed for care should be shared while respecting privacy, age and applicable rules.

This article is for information only and does not replace an assessment by a health professional. If you are worried about a child’s development, talk to their doctor or paediatrician.
