Developmental screening or diagnosis: what is the difference?

Screening and diagnosis are different steps. A brief questionnaire may indicate that a closer look is worthwhile; it does not confirm a condition or replace comprehensive clinical assessment.

By the Talk+ team 18 October 2026 3 min read
Loading reads…

What question does screening answer?

A screening tool is designed to identify children who may benefit from further assessment. Its result depends on the tool, age, setting, language and population for which it was studied. False-positive and false-negative results can occur.

Screening alone does not explain causes, provide a full profile or identify all support needs. A positive result points to a next step to consider; it is not a diagnostic label.

What does diagnostic assessment add?

Clinical assessment combines developmental history, observation and information from caregivers and, when useful, school or other professionals. Methods and tools depend on the clinical question. Autism guidance recommends considering all sources and clinical judgement rather than relying on one tool alone.

A report should explain the question assessed, information considered, conclusion and limitations. If there is not enough information, further observations or follow-up may be appropriate.

What if the family remains concerned?

Share the concern even if screening was negative. If it was positive, ask what referral is recommended and what support can start meanwhile. In Portugal, DGS guidance describes screening and autism assessment within a care pathway; specific procedures depend on the service.

Do not wait for an internet checklist or app to provide a diagnostic answer. Assessment should guide individual decisions, not compare a child with a simplified standard.

In short

  • Screening indicates possible need for further assessment; it does not diagnose.
  • Diagnosis requires integrated information, clinical criteria and professional judgement.

Conclusion

Always ask what kind of tool was used and what happens next. Distinguishing screening from diagnosis helps prevent both premature conclusions and false reassurance.
