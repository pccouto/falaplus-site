CRI: how school and therapy partnerships can work

A Portuguese Centre of Resources for Inclusion (CRI) is a specialist community service that may help a school respond to learning and participation needs. It is not an alternative school or a standard package for every child: it is a partnership shaped by assessment, goals and available resources.

What is a CRI and what can it offer?

Decree-Law 54/2018 describes CRIs as accredited specialist services that support schools’ capacity to promote educational success and inclusion. Teams may include speech and language therapists, occupational therapists, physiotherapists, psychologists or other professionals, depending on the provider and agreement in place.

Support may include direct intervention, observing activities, consultation, training and joint planning. The aim is to bring specialist skills closer to the settings where the child learns and participates, rather than working only on decontextualised goals. Formats and availability are not identical across regions.

The school remains responsible for education

A CRI complements the school; it does not replace teachers, the multidisciplinary team or the support measures organised by the school. Article 18 describes a pedagogical and developmental partnership. Specialist goals should connect to activities and pupil participation, with clear agreement on responsibilities and information-sharing.

Families can ask what goals are in the plan, when and where support happens, how professionals coordinate and what will be reviewed. If support takes place outside class, discuss why, for how long and how learning will transfer to school routines; there is no single model that fits every child.

Building a partnership that makes a difference

The Directorate-General for Education’s CRI guidance looks at the support cycle and the interaction between the pupil and their environments. A brief meeting using concrete school examples and the child’s priorities can help set useful goals and reduce conflicting messages between adults.

Portugal’s 2026 evaluation of inclusive education reports stronger collaboration as well as unequal access to and management of resources and challenges in cross-sector coordination. A CRI should therefore be described as a resource provided for in the system, not a guarantee that every school has the same professionals or hours.

  • Agree on a functional goal and a simple way to review it.
  • Set communication channels and respect confidentiality.
  • Include the child and family in decisions in an age-appropriate way.
  • Ask the school how to access support and where to raise questions.

In summary

Frequently asked questions

Is this support the same for every pupil?

No. Support should reflect each pupil’s goals, barriers and preferences, and be reviewed in light of what happens in school.

Who can answer questions?

Start with the class teacher and ask who coordinates the support and when it will be reviewed. The multidisciplinary team may take part in the processes set out in Portuguese law.

This article is for information only and does not replace an assessment by a health professional. If you are worried about a child’s development, talk to their doctor or paediatrician.
