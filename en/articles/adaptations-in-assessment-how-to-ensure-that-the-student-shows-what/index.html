Adaptations in assessment: how to ensure that students show what they know

An assessment must allow the student to demonstrate the learning it intends to measure. For some students, communication, attention, writing, sensory or motor barriers interfere with the usual format. The Portuguese framework provides for accommodations and adaptations according to the measures mobilized and the applicable rules.

Adjust access without losing the objective

Depending on the need and the evidence, additional time, segmented instructions, an accessible format, supporting technology or an alternative mode of response may be considered. The choice must correspond to the identified barrier and be prepared in advance. An adaptation does not automatically mean reducing the content or changing what is being evaluated.

External assessments and current rules

DL 62/2023 changed rules on adaptations in the external evaluation process. The school must confirm the current procedures and deadlines for each test and explain the decision to the family. The adaptation needs to be familiar to the student; introducing a new tool on assessment day can create another barrier.

In short

  • The adaptation must allow access to the evaluation and reflect the concrete need.
  • External tests have their own rules and procedures that the school must confirm.

In summary

Timely planning and clear communication help make the assessment fairer and more informative.

Informative content. It does not replace individual assessment or clinical or educational guidance.

Frequently asked questions

Does this theme apply the same to all children?

No. The decision must consider development, preferences, context and individual assessment.

When should I ask for help?

When the difficulty persists, affects communication, learning, safety or well-being, talk to qualified professionals and the school, depending on the context.

Does this information replace an individual assessment?

No. This article is general information. Decisions should be based on the child’s needs, context and assessment by qualified professionals.

Where can families find reliable guidance?

Start with the official sources linked in this article and discuss the child’s situation with the school and relevant qualified professionals.

This article is for information only and does not replace an assessment by a health professional. If you are worried about a child’s development, talk to their doctor or paediatrician.
