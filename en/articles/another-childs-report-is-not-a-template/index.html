Can another child’s report be used for comparison?

Reports about different children are not interchangeable templates. A similar age or diagnosis does not make their needs, results or recommendations equivalent.

By the Talk+ team 18 October 2026 3 min read
Loading reads…

Why do reports differ?

Assessment considers history, strengths and difficulties, language, health, setting and the reason for referral. Even children with the same diagnosis may have different profiles and support needs.

Can scores and recommendations be compared?

A score depends on the tool, testing conditions and professional interpretation. Copying goals or supports from another report may create unsuitable expectations and distract from this child’s priorities.

How can privacy be protected?

Do not share identifiable reports in groups or on social media. If an example helps you form questions, remove personal details and ask a professional whether the comparison is relevant. Each report needs to be explained in context.

In short

  • Another child’s report cannot predict this child’s profile or needs.
  • Comparisons can create mistaken expectations and expose private information.

Conclusion

Use another report only to understand what questions to ask, never as a diagnostic standard or plan to copy. Request an individual explanation and protect clinical information.
