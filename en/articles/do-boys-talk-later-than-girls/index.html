Do boys talk later than girls?

“He’s a boy, boys talk later.” This line is heard often at appointments, nurseries and family meals. There is some truth in it, but it does not explain a speech delay or justify putting off an assessment.

By the Talk+ team 25 October 2026 3 min read
Loading reads…

Is it true that boys talk later? In this article we summarise what studies say about differences between boys and girls in language development and why a speech delay in a boy deserves the same attention.

In short

  • On average, girls are slightly ahead with first words and sentences.
  • The difference is small and the overlap huge: many boys talk early and many girls late.
  • Being a boy is linked to a higher chance of late talking, but does not explain it.
  • The reference milestones are the same for boys and girls.

What do studies say?

A study of children aged 8 to 30 months in 10 European language communities found a small average advantage for girls in early communication, vocabulary and word combinations; sex explained only a small part of the differences between children (Eriksson and colleagues, 2012).

In a large Australian study, being a boy was among the factors linked to a higher chance of late language emergence at 24 months, together with a family history of language difficulties (Zubrick and colleagues, 2007). In other words: it is a risk factor, not a reassuring explanation.

What can be said

  • There is a small average difference in favour of girls.
  • Late talking is somewhat more common in boys.
  • Differences between children of the same sex are much larger.

What cannot be said

  • “He’s a boy, so not talking at 2 is normal.”
  • “Boys have different milestones.”
  • “With a boy, it is better to wait longer before assessing.”

When to seek help, boy or girl

  • At 18 months, no words and no pointing to show things.
  • At 2 years, fewer than 50 words or no two-word combinations.
  • At 3 years, people outside the family cannot understand most of what they say.
  • At any age, losing words they used to say or seeming not to understand.

Frequently asked questions about boys and speech

Do boys talk later?

On average, slightly. But the difference is small and does not explain a marked delay.

A 2-year-old boy is not talking. Could it be because he is a boy?

Being a boy does not explain having no words at 2. It is worth talking to a doctor and asking for an assessment.

Are speech milestones different for boys?

No. The warning signs are the same for boys and girls.

Is speech delay more common in boys?

Yes, late talking and some language disorders are somewhat more frequent in boys.

This article is for information only and does not replace an assessment by a health professional. If you are worried about a child’s development, talk to their doctor or paediatrician.
