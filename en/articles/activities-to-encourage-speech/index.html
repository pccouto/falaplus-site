Activities to encourage speech: 12 play ideas for home

You do not need special materials. The richest moments for language are often the simplest: an attentive adult, a game the child enjoys and time to talk.

By the Talk+ team 14 October 2026 4 min read
Loading reads…

Looking for activities to encourage speech? We have put together 12 ideas to try at home and, even more importantly, how to play so that conversation happens naturally, without turning play into a lesson.

In short

  • What matters most is not which game you choose, but how you play: follow the child, comment and wait.
  • Every response counts: a gesture, a sound, pointing, a picture or a word.
  • Many short moments throughout the day are worth more than one long session.
  • If you are worried about speech, these ideas do not replace an assessment.

Before the ideas: 5 golden rules

  1. Follow the child’s interest. If they love cars, talk about cars.
  2. Get down to their level, face to face, so they can see your face.
  3. Comment more, ask less. «The dog is running!» invites more than «What’s this?».
  4. Wait. Count to five in your head before speaking again.
  5. Expand. If they say «ball», reply «big ball!» or «throw the ball!». Do not ask them to repeat it.

12 play activities to encourage language

  • 1. BubblesBlow and stop. Wait for the child to ask for «more», with a word, a gesture or a look.
  • 2. Mystery bagTake objects out of a bag one at a time: «What could it be? A… spoon!»
  • 3. Pretend kitchen«Are you hungry? Would you like soup? Careful, it’s hot!»
  • 4. Action songsPause before the last word and let the child finish it.
  • 5. Books to point atYou do not need to read every word: point, name and make the sounds.
  • 6. My turn, your turnStacking blocks in turns or rolling a ball: turn-taking is the basis of conversation.
  • 7. Choices«Would you like the apple or the banana?» Show both. Choosing is communicating.
  • 8. Animal and car sounds«Moo», «vroom», «tweet»: often among the very first «words».
  • 9. PuppetsA puppet can be scared, sleepy or hungry: great for talking about feelings.
  • 10. Talking routinesAt bath time or getting dressed, describe what you do: «One sock, two socks!»
  • 11. Hide-and-seek with toys«Where’s teddy? Under the table? Inside the box?»
  • 12. Family photosLooking at photos and talking about who is in them helps children tell what happened.

Which activities for which age?

Every idea works across several ages. As a starting point, always adjusted to the child’s interests rather than their age on paper:

AgeGood activities to start with
Babies, around 1 yearbubbles (1), action songs (4), books to point at (5), my turn, your turn (6), animal and car sounds (8)
Toddlers, around 2 yearsmystery bag (2), choices (7), talking routines (10), hide-and-seek with toys (11)
3 years and olderpretend kitchen (3), puppets (9), family photos (12) and making up stories together

What about tablet games?

A game that says the names of things and shows the picture can be a good reason to talk, if an adult plays alongside: naming, commenting and laughing with the child. On their own, picture-choosing games mostly practise understanding and attention; they do not practise the child’s own speech. If you use an app, choose one with no ads: fewer interruptions and fewer distractions. See Benefits of educational games.

What to avoid

Try

  • Repeating the word correctly: «Yes, it’s a dog!»
  • Leaving pauses so the child can join in.
  • Valuing gestures and pictures as communication.

Avoid

  • Turning play into a test: «Say it: DOG!»
  • Correcting every mispronounced word.
  • Talking non-stop.

Frequently asked questions about encouraging speech

How much time a day should I spend on these activities?

Rather than long sessions, what counts is many short moments throughout the day, in routines and in play.

The child does not copy me. What should I do?

Try copying them instead. Imitating the child’s sounds and actions often sparks interest and starts the exchange.

How can I encourage an autistic child to talk?

The same principles apply: follow the child’s interests, respond to any form of communication and give time. Gestures, pictures and AAC are valid communication and do not stop speech. Every autistic child has a different profile, so guidance from a speech and language therapist helps choose goals and supports. See Augmentative and alternative communication.

Do these activities replace speech therapy?

No. They are everyday ideas. If you are worried about speech, talk to the child’s doctor. See also Speech delay.

This article is for information only and does not replace an assessment by a health professional. If you are worried about a child’s development, talk to their doctor or paediatrician.
