How to choose and compare therapies for a child

Different therapies address different questions: communicating, moving, joining routines, managing anxiety or eating safely. How do the main fields differ?

Speech and language therapy addresses communication, language and speech and, depending on training, feeding and swallowing. Occupational therapy focuses on everyday activities and participation; physiotherapy on movement and mobility. Psychology addresses mental health and behaviour, while music or art therapy use creative processes for their own therapeutic goals. The table summarises common areas, not every therapy available; professional roles and boundaries vary by country and training.

What should families ask before starting?

What matters most to the child? What assessment supports the proposal? What activities will happen and who provides them? How are consent, communication and safety respected? What outcome will be observed, in which setting and when will the plan be reviewed? How will clinicians and school coordinate? The child should be able to show enjoyment, refusal or need for a break. Frequently asked questions

Should a child receive every available therapy?

No. Support should match needs and priorities, leaving time for rest, play and family life.

How can I tell if a therapy is helping?

Agree observable goals and a review date; consider function, wellbeing and participation, not attendance alone.
