DLD and sport: understanding to participate

The connection between DLD and sport becomes visible when game instructions require understanding many words and actions at once.

What can make participation harder with DLD and sport?

Developmental language disorder can affect understanding and expression. The NIDCD warns that failing to follow an instruction may reflect difficulty understanding language and can be mistaken for misbehaviour. (NIDCD)

During training, check what was understood before concluding that a child is uninterested. Performance in a game cannot diagnose a language disorder.

How can instructions be made more accessible?

Whittington Health materials recommend shorter instructions, visual support, concrete examples and time to process information. This guidance was prepared for schools; here we suggest adapting it to coaching without claiming demonstrated sporting effectiveness. (Whittington Health NHS)

For example, try “Pass the ball to Marta” with a demonstration before adding “Run to the cone”. To check understanding, invite the child to show the first step without putting them on the spot in front of the group.

How can children make themselves understood?

As a practical suggestion, agree ways to request help, clarify a rule, refuse or ask for a break. Accept speech, gestures and pictures. If a child uses a communication system, maintain access during the activity.

Share familiar strategies with the coach. When language difficulties affect daily life, a speech and language therapist (speech-language pathologist) can assess needs and guide support. (NIDCD) The training goal is to provide opportunities to understand and participate.

What can we say — and what can we not say?

What we can say

  • Instruction language can be adapted and communication supported.

What we cannot say

  • Not following a rule proves disobedience or sport treats DLD.

Frequently asked questions

Can children with DLD take part in sport?

Yes. Adjust communication and tasks to individual needs and listen to the child’s preferences.

How should instructions be given to a child with DLD?

Use short sentences, one action at a time, demonstrations and processing time. Check understanding respectfully.

Does not following a rule mean a lack of attention?

Not necessarily. The rule may not have been understood; other factors can also affect the response.

Does sport replace speech and language therapy?

No. It can offer opportunities for participation but does not replace assessment or specialist support.
