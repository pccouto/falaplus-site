Speech delay: the child says single words but does not combine them

“Water”, “mummy”, “car”, “no”. The child already has some words but still uses them one at a time. Joining two words (“more water”, “car gone”) is an important step, because it marks the beginning of grammar.

By the Talk+ team 25 October 2026 3 min read

When a child is not combining words around age 2, it may be a speech or language delay that deserves attention. In this article we explain when two-word phrases appear, what may be getting in the way and how to help. See also Speech delay at 2 years.

In short

  • Many children start joining two words between 18 and 24 months, once they have about 50 words.
  • Not combining two words at 24 months is one of the guidelines used for assessment.
  • Having more, and more varied, words (actions, not just names) makes combinations easier.
  • Expanding what the child says by adding one word is the most useful strategy at home.

When do first sentences appear?

Milestones revised by the American Academy of Pediatrics state that by age 2 most children say at least two words together, such as “more milk” (Zubler and colleagues, 2022). In research, not combining words at 24 months, like having fewer than 50 words, is used as a criterion for late talking (Rescorla, 1989).

  • Asking for more“More water”, “more ball”.
  • Refusing or finishing“No want”, “all gone”.
  • Who does what“Daddy gone”, “baby sleep”.
  • Whose it is“Brother car”, “mummy shoe”.
  • Where it is“Ball here”, “cat there”.

Why isn’t the child combining words?

  • They still have few words: combinations often appear after a rapid growth in vocabulary.
  • They mostly use names of things and few action words or “useful” words like “more”, “no”, “gone”.
  • They find it hard to produce sequences of sounds (see Apraxia of speech).
  • They use long memorised phrases but do not create new combinations (see Gestalt language processing).
  • There is a language difficulty that deserves assessment (see Speech delay and DLD).

How to help combine words

  1. Expand one word: if they say “water”, reply “More water!” or “Cold water!”.
  2. Teach key words that combine with everything: “more”, “no”, “gone”, “here”, “open”.
  3. Use action words in routines: “eat”, “wash”, “jump”, “sleep”.
  4. Pause in familiar phrases: “More…”, waiting for the child to finish.
  5. Use pictures or gestures to show that words go together, for example two cards side by side.

Frequently asked questions about combining words

When should a child start combining words?

Many start between 18 and 24 months. By age 2, most join at least two words.

The child is 2 and only says single words. Is it a speech delay?

It may be. Not combining words at 24 months is a guideline for asking for an assessment.

How can I teach a child to make sentences?

By expanding what they say, teaching words like “more” and “gone”, and using action words in routines.

The child repeats whole phrases from cartoons. Does that count?

It shows memory and interest, but the therapist will look at whether the child also creates new combinations.

This article is for information only and does not replace an assessment by a health professional. If you are worried about a child’s development, talk to their doctor or paediatrician.
