Physiotherapy at school: mobility, comfort and participation

To join a field trip, take part in PE or simply move between classrooms, a child may need physical access, equipment, time and coordinated support. School physiotherapy should connect motor skills with routines and choices that matter to the pupil.

The focus is function in the school context

Physiotherapy may support mobility, positioning, transfers, access to spaces and safe participation. Planning starts with functional goals agreed with the child, family and school. Improving a movement outside its context may not remove the barrier if a corridor remains inaccessible or an activity allows only one way to take part.

A physiotherapist may observe routines, advise the team, trial assistive products and collaborate with other professionals. Support should not assume that pupils with the same diagnosis need the same help, or that participation depends on walking unaided.

PE, break time and trips

Physical activities can be adapted while keeping the learning objective and offering meaningful participation: change rules, equipment, distance, group role or breaks. Before a trip, the school can check routes, toilets, ramps, transport and access to shared spaces.

Safety does not mean automatically excluding the pupil. Assess specific risks and reasonable ways to reduce them, involving the student and family. Support needs may change with fatigue, terrain, health and activity.

Planning and review

Portugal’s Decree-Law 54/2018 organises measures around participation and learning and provides for collaboration with specialist community resources when needed. How school physiotherapy is delivered varies between schools and regions; families can ask about local arrangements and links with the health team.

Agree on an observable goal — reaching the library, joining an adapted activity or completing an agreed transfer — then review whether the space and support make it possible with dignity and safety.

  • Ask the pupil which activities they want to do.
  • Map physical and organisational barriers before choosing a solution.
  • Plan breaks and support levels without removing learning opportunities.
  • Review the plan when routines, equipment or health needs change.

In short

  • School physiotherapy connects movement with real school activities.
  • Participation may require changes to spaces, tasks and support, not only individual practice.

In summary

An accessible school plans activities so mobility, safety and belonging can coexist.

Informational content. It does not replace individual assessment or legal or educational advice.

Frequently asked questions

Is this support the same for every pupil?

No. Support should reflect each pupil’s goals, barriers and preferences, and be reviewed in light of what happens in school.

Who can answer questions?

Start with the class teacher and ask who coordinates the support and when it will be reviewed. The multidisciplinary team may take part in the processes set out in Portuguese law.

This article is for information only and does not replace an assessment by a health professional. If you are worried about a child’s development, talk to their doctor or paediatrician.
