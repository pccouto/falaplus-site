Multimodal communication: gestures and signs before verbal speech

Communication begins before the first words and does not depend solely on verbal speech. Gestures, expressions, gaze, vocalizations, signs and images allow the child to share attention, ask, refuse and comment. Recognizing these means expands communication and does not require waiting for speech.

Read and respond to communicative attempts

Pointing, extending a hand, moving an object away, alternately looking or using a conventional gesture may have communicative intent. The adult can observe the context, attribute meaning prudently and respond verbally, without requiring the child to repeat. Gestures must be respected even when speech is developing.

Combine modes without hierarchy

Speaking while using a gesture, sign, image, or AAC system provides accessible models. The choice of resources depends on the child's assessment and preferences. Augmentative systems can coexist with speech and should not be removed to test whether the child can communicate orally.

In short

  • Multimodal communication recognizes multiple ways of expressing meaning.
  • Responding and modeling supports interaction without requiring a unique form.

In summary

When adults value each attempt, the child finds more opportunities to be understood and participate.

Frequently asked questions

Does this theme apply the same to all children?

No. The decision must consider development, preferences, context and individual assessment.

When should I ask for help?

When the difficulty persists, affects communication, learning, safety or well-being, talk to qualified professionals and the school, depending on the context.

Does this information replace an individual assessment?

No. This article is general information. Decisions should be based on the child’s needs, context and assessment by qualified professionals.

Where can families find reliable guidance?

Start with the official sources linked in this article and discuss the child’s situation with the school and relevant qualified professionals.

This article is for information only and does not replace an assessment by a health professional. If you are worried about a child’s development, talk to their doctor or paediatrician.
