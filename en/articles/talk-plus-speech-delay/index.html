When a speech delay is a concern, the first question is usually what to do. The answer combines assessment, everyday conversation and chances to play. Talk Plus can be one of those chances, without replacing professional support.

In short

  • If you are worried, seek an assessment: waiting is not the only option.
  • Daily interaction is the main place children learn language.
  • Talk Plus complements this with pictures, voice and games; it does not assess or treat.

What should you do first about a speech delay?

Talk to the pediatrician or a speech-language pathologist and ask for a hearing check. Bring concrete examples: words used, gestures, what the child understands.

A delay alone does not reveal its cause. Our speech delay guide covers the warning signs.

What can help at home?

Some simple habits, adapted to the child’s age and interests:

  • Talk about what the child is seeing or doing.
  • Pause for a few seconds so the child can respond.
  • Repeat the child’s idea and add one word.
  • Value gestures, pointing and pictures: they are communication too.

Where does an app like Talk Plus fit?

An app can add chances to hear words and ask for things with pictures, especially with an adult playing alongside. It does not replace conversation or speech therapy.

What we can say

  • Play and conversation offer language opportunities.
  • An assessment helps show what support is needed.

What we cannot say

  • An app makes a child talk.
  • It is enough to wait and see.

Frequently asked questions

Is it normal for a 2 year old not to talk?

Children vary widely, but if a child uses very few words it is worth speaking to a pediatrician or speech-language pathologist. There is no need to wait.

Does Talk Plus treat speech delay?

No. It is an app for play and communication that can complement a child’s support.

Can speech delay be autism?

A delay alone does not tell you that. Only a professional assessment can clarify it, and support should not be postponed.

Should the child use the app alone?

They can, but an available adult creates more chances to talk. It is not a condition for playing.

This article is for information only and does not replace an assessment by a health professional. If you are worried about a child’s development, talk to their doctor or paediatrician.
