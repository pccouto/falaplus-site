Pausing, stopping or changing therapy does not necessarily mean giving up. It may be a planned decision when goals are met, needs change, benefit is absent, distress occurs or the plan is no longer sustainable.

Which signs call for review?

Review when a goal is met, remains unchanged despite appropriate delivery, is no longer a priority or creates disproportionate burden. Persistent fear, pain, reduced participation and conflict between therapies also need prompt attention.

How should you discuss this with the clinician?

Ask to review goals, data, barriers, alternatives and the risks of continuing or stopping. Consider whether method, frequency, clinician or setting could fit better. A good transition gives clear information without blaming the child or family.

How can a break or discharge be planned?

Record what helped, useful strategies, signs that warrant reassessment and who to contact. When appropriate, taper support or arrange a future review. If changing professionals, share only necessary and authorised information for continuity.

Frequently asked questions

Can I stop without formal discharge?

You can request review and make an informed decision; urgent safety concerns require immediate action.

Will changing clinicians harm the child?

A transition may require adjustment, but remaining in an unsuitable plan also has costs.

Must a break be justified?

It helps to record reasons, plans and review criteria without making the family prove the decision.

When should therapy restart?

Agree concrete signs or a review date according to need.

This article is for information only and does not replace an assessment by a health professional. If you are worried about a child’s development, talk to their doctor or paediatrician.
