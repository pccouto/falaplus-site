Speech delay: warning signs by age

Every child has their own pace, but there are signs it is best not to let slip. This checklist summarises, by age, the signs that mean it is worth talking to your GP or paediatrician and asking for an assessment.

By the Talk+ team 25 October 2026 3 min read
Loading reads…

Speech delay warning signs are not for diagnosing, but they help decide when to ask for help. Use this list as a guide and take it to the appointment. For an overview, see Speech delay.

In short

  • Warning signs include speech, but also gestures, understanding and interaction.
  • A single sign does not mean there is a problem, but it is a reason to watch and talk to a doctor.
  • Loss of words or skills, at any age, calls for a prompt assessment.
  • When in doubt, assessing is safer than waiting.

Warning signs by age

AgeWarning signs
12 monthsNo varied babbling (“ba-ba”, “da-da”), no response to name, no pointing or waving.
18 monthsNo words at all, no pointing to show, does not understand familiar words.
24 monthsFewer than 50 words or no two-word combinations; only repeats without using words to ask.
3 yearsMostly single words; outsiders cannot understand most of what they say; no questions.
4 yearsVery short or disorganised sentences; cannot say what happened; difficulty following instructions.
5 yearsStill hard to understand; difficulty telling stories or chatting with other children.
Any ageLoses words or skills they had; does not seem to hear well; gets very frustrated at not being understood.

These guidelines are based on milestones revised by the American Academy of Pediatrics and on clinical practice described by the American Speech-Language-Hearing Association (Zubler and colleagues, 2022; ASHA). For more detail, see Milestones from 0 to 24 months and Milestones from 2 to 5.

Beyond words: other signs to watch

  • Little eye contact or little sharing of interest with the adult.
  • Little imitation of gestures, sounds or actions.
  • Very repetitive or limited play for their age.
  • Difficulty chewing, lots of drooling or trouble coordinating the mouth.
  • Frequent ear infections or a sense that they “hear when they want to”.

What to do when there are warning signs

  1. Write down what you notice and when (words, gestures, understanding).
  2. Book an appointment with your GP or paediatrician and take the list.
  3. Ask for a hearing assessment and a speech and language therapy referral.
  4. Start using simple strategies at home now (see 8 strategies for home).

Frequently asked questions about speech delay warning signs

What are the main signs of speech delay?

No words at 18 months, fewer than 50 words or no two-word phrases at 2, and unclear speech at 3 are among the most used.

Is a single warning sign a cause for concern?

Not necessarily, but it is a reason to watch and talk to a doctor.

Should I wait for the routine check-up?

If there are warning signs, there is no need to wait. You can book a specific appointment.

Are warning signs different for bilingual children?

No. Bilingual children reach milestones across their languages combined. A delay in all languages deserves assessment.

This article is for information only and does not replace an assessment by a health professional. If you are worried about a child’s development, talk to their doctor or paediatrician.
