DLD: teaching vocabulary across lessons

Teaching vocabulary with DLD means making explicit the words that can block access to an explanation, story or problem.

Teaching vocabulary with DLD means making explicit the words that can block access to an explanation, story or problem.

What supports this approach?

The NIDCD describes difficulties learning words and understanding instructions in developmental language disorder. Difficulty responding can reflect a language barrier. (NIDCD)

Whittington Health recommends explaining new words, concrete examples, visual support and processing time. Strategies need to be selected for the individual child and task. (Whittington Health NHS)

How can it be tried in practice?

The following steps are examples to adapt to the learner and context; they have not been evaluated as an intervention programme.

  1. Before a lesson, select a few essential words, such as “root”, “stem” and “leaf”.
  2. Introduce each word with a simple explanation and an object or picture. Show different examples of the same concept.
  3. Invite the child to locate or use the word in a supported activity. Repeating it alone does not demonstrate understanding.
  4. Revisit words in another lesson and situation. Allow response time and accept pointing, selecting or explaining.

How can progress be reviewed?

Observe whether the child recognises the concept in a new example and uses it with less help. Coordinate with supporting professionals without turning a lesson into a clinical assessment.

What can and cannot be concluded?

What we can say

  • Explaining and revisiting words can make curriculum language more accessible.

What we cannot say

  • A memorised list proves understanding or a vocabulary activity treats DLD.

Frequently asked questions

How many words should be taught at once?

There is no universal number. Choose a small set and adjust according to understanding and use in context.

Does repeating a word mean understanding it?

Not necessarily. Check meaning through examples, choices and different situations.

How can I tell whether the approach helps?

Define an observable task, record the help needed and revisit understanding or application later. Speed alone does not measure learning.

Do these suggestions replace individual assessment?

No. Review adjustments with the learner and relevant professionals when difficulties persist or concerning changes appear.

This article is for information only and does not replace an assessment by a health professional. If you are worried about a child’s development, talk to their doctor or paediatrician.
