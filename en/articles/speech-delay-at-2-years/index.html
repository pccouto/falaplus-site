Speech delay at 2 years: the child says very few words

Age 2 is when most families wonder about speech delay. There is a good reason: this is when vocabulary usually “explodes” and first sentences appear. When that does not happen, it is worth taking a closer look.

By the Talk+ team 25 October 2026 3 min read
Loading reads…

A 2-year-old who says very few words is often called a “late talker”. In this article we explain the guidelines professionals use for speech delay at 2 years, what helps predict progress and what you can do right now. See also Speech delay: warning signs.

In short

  • A widely used guideline: fewer than 50 words or no two-word phrases at 24 months.
  • About one in eight children are in this situation at age 2.
  • Many catch up, but it is impossible to know for sure who will; so assessment and monitoring are recommended.
  • Good understanding, lots of gestures and good interaction are favourable signs.

The guidelines: 50 words and two words together

One of the most widely used research criteria is a child having, at 24 months, fewer than 50 words or not combining two words (“more water”, “daddy gone”) (Rescorla, 1989). Milestones revised by the American Academy of Pediatrics state that by age 2 most children say at least two words together and point to pictures in a book when asked (Zubler and colleagues, 2022).

In a large Australian study, about 13% of children were late talkers at 24 months (Zubrick and colleagues, 2007).

What helps predict progress?

More favourable signsSigns that need more attention
Understands well what is saidDifficulty understanding simple instructions
Uses many gestures and pointsFew gestures, little pointing
Seeks out the adult to play and shareLittle interaction or eye contact
Imitates sounds and wordsRarely imitates
No family history of difficultiesRelatives with language or reading difficulties

A review concluded that most late talkers reach the typical range by school age but remain, on average, a little below their peers, and that no single factor predicts well who will catch up (Rescorla, 2011). See Do late talkers always catch up?

What to do now

  1. Talk to your GP or paediatrician and ask for a hearing assessment.
  2. Ask for a speech and language therapy referral if there are warning signs.
  3. Make a list of the words the child says (see How many words should a child say).
  4. Use simple interaction strategies at home (see 8 strategies for home).

Frequently asked questions about speech delay at 2 years

How many words should a 2-year-old say?

Many say 50 or more words and start joining two together. Fewer than 50 words or no combinations at 24 months is a guideline for assessment.

The 2-year-old does not talk but understands everything. Should I worry?

Understanding well is a good sign, but the speech delay should still be assessed and monitored.

Does speech delay at 2 go away on its own?

For many children it does, but no one can know for sure which ones. Waiting without monitoring is not recommended.

What does a speech therapist do with a 2-year-old?

They assess communication and often coach the family to use strategies in everyday life, through play.
