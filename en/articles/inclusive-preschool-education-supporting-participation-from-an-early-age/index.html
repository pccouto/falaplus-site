Inclusive preschool education: supporting early participation

In preschool, playing, exploring, communicating and belonging to a group are central dimensions of learning. An inclusive response observes what facilitates or hinders participation in routines and adapts the environment, without expecting all children to learn or communicate in the same way.

Observe the child in context

The team can observe entrances, play, meals, transitions and group activities. The objective is to understand interests, skills and barriers and experiment with accessible support: visual choices, response time, materials within reach or different ways of communicating. An observation does not equate to a diagnosis.

Partnership with family and services

The family knows routines and signs of comfort or discomfort and must be able to share priorities. When there are external professionals, the coordination must have clear objectives and appropriate consent. In Portugal, measures to support learning and inclusion cover different levels of education, with organization adjusted to the context.

In short

  • The environment and routines can be adjusted to encourage each child’s participation.
  • Observation and partnership help identify useful supports without labeling.

In summary

Small changes in routine can expand opportunities to play, communicate and learn together.

Informative content. It does not replace individual assessment or clinical or educational guidance.

Frequently asked questions

Does this theme apply the same to all children?

No. The decision must consider development, preferences, context and individual assessment.

When should I ask for help?

When the difficulty persists, affects communication, learning, safety or well-being, talk to qualified professionals and the school, depending on the context.

Does this information replace an individual assessment?

No. This article is general information. Decisions should be based on the child’s needs, context and assessment by qualified professionals.

Where can families find reliable guidance?

Start with the official sources linked in this article and discuss the child’s situation with the school and relevant qualified professionals.

This article is for information only and does not replace an assessment by a health professional. If you are worried about a child’s development, talk to their doctor or paediatrician.
