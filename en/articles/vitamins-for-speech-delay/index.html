Vitamins for speech delay: do they work?

Online, there is no shortage of vitamins and supplements promising to «help children talk». The scientific evidence does not support any of them as a treatment for speech delay. There are, however, deficiencies a doctor needs to know about.

By the Talk+ team 24 October 2026 3 min read
Loading reads…

People searching for vitamins for speech delay want to help the child as quickly as possible. In this article we separate what has been shown from what has not: folinic acid in autism, vitamin B12 deficiency in babies and what to do before giving any supplement.

In short

  • No vitamin or supplement has been shown to treat speech delay in general.
  • A trial of folinic acid in autistic children with language impairment had positive but preliminary results, not replicated at scale.
  • Severe vitamin B12 deficiency in babies can cause developmental delay or regression; it is rare and diagnosed with blood tests.
  • Supplements only on medical advice. Communication support should not wait for them.

Are there vitamins that help a child talk?

No study has shown that a vitamin, mineral or supplement treats speech delay in a child without a deficiency. Language development depends on hearing, brain development and many opportunities for interaction.

What about folinic acid in autism?

A randomised trial with 48 autistic children with language impairment, aged 3 to 14, compared folinic acid with placebo for 12 weeks. The folinic acid group improved more in verbal communication (Frye et al., 2018). The authors themselves considered the results preliminary, and the trial registry notes that the sponsor could not fully validate the data. It is therefore not a recommendation for all children.

When a deficiency can matter

Severe vitamin B12 deficiency in babies, described mainly in breastfed babies of mothers with a deficiency (for example, on vegan diets without a supplement), can cause low muscle tone, feeding difficulties and developmental delay or regression, usually between 2 and 12 months. It is rare, diagnosed with blood tests and treated under medical guidance; the earlier, the better the outlook.

Talk to a doctor, not the pharmacy, if the child:

  • has lost skills they already had (words, gestures, smiles);
  • has feeding difficulties, poor weight gain or has become floppier;
  • follows a restrictive diet, or the breastfeeding mother follows a vegan diet without a B12 supplement.

What we know

  • Severe deficiencies (such as B12) affect development and must be treated.
  • Folinic acid has preliminary results in a specific group of autistic children.
  • What helps language most is interaction and, where needed, speech therapy.

Myths

  • «There is a vitamin that makes children talk.»
  • «Natural supplements have no risks.»
  • «Try the supplement first, then therapy.»

What to do instead of looking for a supplement

  1. Ask the doctor for a developmental and hearing assessment.
  2. If a deficiency is suspected, blood tests and any supplement are decided by the doctor.
  3. Start communication support at home now: talk, read, play and wait for the child’s response.
  4. Be wary of products promising to «unlock speech».

The same reasoning applies to diets presented as a solution: see Does a gluten-free diet treat autism?.

Frequently asked questions about vitamins and speech

What is the best vitamin for speech delay?

No vitamin has been shown to treat speech delay. If a deficiency is suspected, the doctor will order blood tests.

Does vitamin B12 help speech delay?

Only when there is a deficiency. Severe deficiency in babies can delay development and must be treated by a doctor.

Does omega-3 help a child talk?

There is no evidence that omega-3 treats speech delay.

Is there medicine for speech delay?

There is no medicine for speech delay. Support comes from assessment, interaction at home and, where needed, speech therapy.

This article is for information only and does not replace an assessment by a health professional. If you are worried about a child’s development, talk to their doctor or paediatrician.
