Gamification and neuroplasticity: what structured video games can teach

Neuroplasticity describes the ability of the nervous system to change with experience. Digital games can offer structured practice, feedback and repetition, but they are not automatic brain activation nor do they guarantee that a skill will carry over into daily life.

What a game can offer

A well-designed game can make practice motivating, adjust difficulty and give immediate feedback on a concrete task. Reviews of gamified interventions find promising results for specific goals, but studies vary and do not demonstrate that any video game changes the brain in any broad way. Preference for a game does not prove learning or clinical improvement.

How to assess whether it's worth it

Set an observable skill, track progress off-screen, and confirm your child remains interested and comfortable. Combine play with interaction, play and real activities. Take breaks and consider sensory and sleep needs. Games do not replace therapy, teaching or individual assessment.

In short

  • Games can support the practice of specific skills, with evidence still variable.
  • Transfer to everyday contexts needs to be observed, not assumed.

In summary

Choose the tool based on the child's objective and experience, and evaluate what changes beyond the screen.

Frequently asked questions

Does this theme apply the same to all children?

No. The decision must consider development, preferences, context and individual assessment.

When should I ask for help?

When the difficulty persists, affects communication, learning, safety or well-being, talk to qualified professionals and the school, depending on the context.

Does this information replace an individual assessment?

No. This article is general information. Decisions should be based on the child’s needs, context and assessment by qualified professionals.

Where can families find reliable guidance?

Start with the official sources linked in this article and discuss the child’s situation with the school and relevant qualified professionals.
