Prevent bullying and protect the participation of neurodivergent students

Neurodivergent students may face exclusion, harassment or misunderstanding, but it should not be assumed that this happens to everyone. The school must take reports and changes in behavior seriously, guarantee safety and act in accordance with applicable protection and coexistence procedures.

Listen without questioning or blaming

Ask the student how they prefer to communicate and what support they need to feel safe. Record observable facts, dates and locations; avoid demanding that the child confront whoever hurt him. The response should consider communication, sensory differences, peer relationships and possible barriers to reporting.

Daily prevention and consistent response

Clear rules against bullying, supervision of less structured spaces, teaching respect for differences and accessible reporting channels help prevent this. When there is an incident, the school must explain who is following, what protective measures were taken and when it will review the situation, preserving the privacy of those involved.

In short

  • Student safety and voice should guide the response to bullying situations.
  • Prevention requires whole-school practices, not just intervention after an incident.

In summary

A safe school makes it possible to ask for help and participate without having to hide needs or differences.

Informative content. It does not replace individual assessment or clinical or educational guidance.

Frequently asked questions

Does this theme apply the same to all children?

No. The decision must consider development, preferences, context and individual assessment.

When should I ask for help?

When the difficulty persists, affects communication, learning, safety or well-being, talk to qualified professionals and the school, depending on the context.

Does this information replace an individual assessment?

No. This article is general information. Decisions should be based on the child’s needs, context and assessment by qualified professionals.

Where can families find reliable guidance?

Start with the official sources linked in this article and discuss the child’s situation with the school and relevant qualified professionals.

This article is for information only and does not replace an assessment by a health professional. If you are worried about a child’s development, talk to their doctor or paediatrician.
