Speech delay and nursery: does nursery help a child talk?

“Send them to nursery, they’ll start talking in no time with the other children.” Many families hear this advice. Nursery can be a good setting for language, but it does not, on its own, fix a speech delay.

By the Talk+ team 25 October 2026 3 min read
Loading reads…

Does nursery help a child talk? In this article we look at what research says about childcare and language, why the quality of interaction matters more than simply being around other children and how family and nursery staff can work together on a speech delay. See also Speech delay.

In short

  • Being around other children does not, on its own, fix a speech delay.
  • What counts most is the quality of interaction with attentive adults, at home and at nursery.
  • Staff who chat, wait and expand what the child says are great allies.
  • Nursery does not replace assessment or speech therapy when they are needed.

What do studies show?

A large US study followed more than a thousand children and found that the quality of care, especially how much adults stimulated language, was linked to better cognitive and language development; time spent in childcare on its own mattered less, and the family environment remained the most important factor (NICHD Early Child Care Research Network, 2000).

What can be said

  • A nursery with adults who talk a lot can support language.
  • Playing with other children creates chances to communicate.
  • Nursery staff are valuable observers of development.

What cannot be said

  • “Nursery fixes speech delay.”
  • “Being with other children is enough to start talking.”
  • “Once at nursery there is no need to assess.”

How nursery staff can help

  • Chat with the child one to one, even for a few minutes, throughout the day.
  • Wait for a response, accepting gestures and sounds.
  • Expand what the child says and name what they are doing.
  • Use visual supports in room routines (see Visual routines).
  • Share observations with the family and the speech and language therapist.

Family and nursery working together

  1. Share your concern with nursery staff and ask what they notice.
  2. Agree on common words, signs or pictures for home and nursery.
  3. If there are warning signs, ask for an assessment without waiting to see if nursery helps (see Warning signs).
  4. If there is speech therapy, ask for strategies to reach nursery too (see Inclusive preschool education).

Frequently asked questions about speech delay and nursery

Does nursery help a child talk?

It can help if there is lots of quality interaction with adults, but it does not fix a speech delay on its own.

The child talks at home but not at nursery. What could it be?

It may be shyness, settling in or, if it lasts, anxiety. See the article on selective mutism.

Does nursery cause speech delay?

There is no evidence that nursery by itself causes delay. What counts is the quality of interaction in every setting.

Should I wait to see if nursery helps before asking for an assessment?

No. If there are warning signs, ask for an assessment at the same time.

This article is for information only and does not replace an assessment by a health professional. If you are worried about a child’s development, talk to their doctor or paediatrician.
