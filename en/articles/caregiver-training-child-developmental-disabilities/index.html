Caregiver training for child development

Structured caregiver programmes can teach strategies to support communication, engagement, daily living skills and positive behaviour in family routines. WHO provides a programme for families of children aged 2–9 with developmental delays or disabilities; it is not a universal therapy or a replacement for specialist services.

What is taught?

WHO’s Caregiver Skills Training combines group sessions and home visits. Topics include engaging the child, play and routines, communication, behaviour, daily living skills and caregiver wellbeing. A facilitator helps families choose goals and practise strategies in real situations, adapting them to priorities and available resources.

What is the family’s role?

Families know their routines and can choose what to try, but they do not have to become the child’s therapist. Support should reduce burden, respect culture, time and mental health, and connect families with services when needed. Ask what training facilitators receive, what supervision is provided and how the programme adapts to the child.

In short

  • Goals depend on individual needs, preferences and assessment.
  • The team should explain the plan and review its effects on participation and wellbeing.

For information only. It does not replace individual assessment or clinical advice.

Frequently asked questions

Does caregiver training replace speech therapy, psychology or other support?

No. It is complementary and should connect with the child’s needs and services.

Must I use strategies all day?

No. A programme should help add realistic opportunities without turning every interaction into a task.

This article is for information only and does not replace an assessment by a health professional. If you are worried about a child’s development, talk to their doctor or paediatrician.
