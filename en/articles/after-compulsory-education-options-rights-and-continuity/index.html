After compulsory education: options, rights and continuity

Compulsory education in Portugal extends until the age of 18 or the completion of secondary education, under legal terms. Leaving school requires preparation and information about training options, supported employment, services and participation in the community, according to the young person's preferences.

Start by knowing what the young person wants

Accessible conversations and practical experiences help identify interests and goals. The young person may need support to communicate choices, discover new environments or compare options. PIT, when applied, must begin early and articulate school, family, and community resources.

Avoid a gap between services

The transition requires knowing who takes each step, what support is still available and how applications or referrals are processed. Access rules vary between programs and may change; the school and the competent services must confirm the current information. There is no single path to success.

In short

  • The transition must be gradual, informed and centered on the young person's choices.
  • Confirming services, deadlines and criteria with the responsible entities avoids incorrect expectations.

In summary

A useful plan transforms an aspiration into a close and safe experience, with responsible parties and a date to review what has been learned.

Informative content. It does not replace individual assessment or clinical or educational guidance.

Frequently asked questions

Does this theme apply the same to all children?

No. The decision must consider development, preferences, context and individual assessment.

When should I ask for help?

When the difficulty persists, affects communication, learning, safety or well-being, talk to qualified professionals and the school, depending on the context.

Does this information replace an individual assessment?

No. This article is general information. Decisions should be based on the child’s needs, context and assessment by qualified professionals.

Where can families find reliable guidance?

Start with the official sources linked in this article and discuss the child’s situation with the school and relevant qualified professionals.

This article is for information only and does not replace an assessment by a health professional. If you are worried about a child’s development, talk to their doctor or paediatrician.
