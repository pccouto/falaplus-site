School psychology and neurodiversity: support without reducing a child to a diagnosis

Anxiety, overload, attention difficulties or peer conflict can affect learning. A school psychologist can help make sense of what is happening and coordinate responses with the child, family and education team — without treating neurological difference as a character flaw.

The school psychologist’s role

Work may include observation, consultation with teachers and family, assessment within professional scope, counselling, mental health promotion and helping the team choose strategies. In Portugal, a psychologist is a permanent member of the EMAEI; involvement of other professionals depends on the child’s needs and the school’s organisation.

Behaviour is information about an interaction, not a diagnosis by itself. Before concluding that a child “doesn’t want to”, the team can explore whether instructions were understood, the task is accessible, or noise, pain, fear, fatigue or exclusion is involved.

Wellbeing and learning belong together

Helpful support sets observable goals: feeling safe enough to ask for a break, gradually returning to an activity, understanding group-work expectations or reducing a barrier that triggers anxiety. The child should participate in planning in a way suited to their age and communication. Strategies should not require masking autistic traits or enduring distress to appear “well adjusted”.

When signs are persistent or intense, school and family can coordinate with health professionals. School support does not replace clinical care, and a clinical diagnosis does not automatically determine a school measure: the educational response considers participation and barriers in context.

Privacy, consent and review

Share only information needed to support the child and use secure channels. The student record is subject to confidentiality and data-protection rules. The team should explain who can access records, how information guides support and when the plan will be reviewed.

Families can bring specific examples and ask what changes will be tried, who will coordinate them and how their effect will be checked. If a child says a strategy feels uncomfortable, that signal deserves to be heard and discussed.

  • Describe events and contexts instead of assuming intentions.
  • Agree on one wellbeing goal and one school-participation goal.
  • Avoid promising that one technique will solve every difficulty.
  • Review support with the child, family and teachers.

In short

  • A school psychologist can help understand needs and coordinate educational and wellbeing support.
  • A diagnosis or behaviour does not replace looking at concrete barriers or listening to the child.

In summary

School psychological support is most useful when it improves safety, participation and learning while respecting identity, privacy and preferences.

Informational content. It does not replace individual assessment or legal or educational advice.

Frequently asked questions

Is this support the same for every pupil?

No. Support should reflect each pupil’s goals, barriers and preferences, and be reviewed in light of what happens in school.

Who can answer questions?

Start with the class teacher and ask who coordinates the support and when it will be reviewed. The multidisciplinary team may take part in the processes set out in Portuguese law.

This article is for information only and does not replace an assessment by a health professional. If you are worried about a child’s development, talk to their doctor or paediatrician.
