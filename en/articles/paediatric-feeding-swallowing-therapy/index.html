Paediatric feeding and swallowing therapy

Eating involves health, nutrition, motor and sensory skills, context and relationships. Coughing or choking, very difficult meals, pain, poor growth or a very limited range of foods warrant assessment; textures or positioning should not be changed without clinical guidance.

Who assesses, and what may happen?

Assessment may involve paediatrics, a speech and language therapist trained in swallowing, dietetics, occupational therapy and other specialists depending on the cause. Clinicians consider medical history, posture, chewing, coordination and mealtime context. Suspected pharyngeal swallowing problems may require instrumental tests, decided and conducted by a qualified team.

What goals may be set?

A plan may aim for safe, nutritionally adequate meals, participation and less distress. Strategies depend on diagnosis and may include individual adaptations, skill practice, caregiver support or medical treatment. Food should not be forced. Recurrent coughing, breathing changes, choking or respiratory infections should be shared promptly with the health team.

In short

  • Goals depend on individual needs, preferences and assessment.
  • The team should explain the plan and review its effects on participation and wellbeing.

For information only. It does not replace individual assessment or clinical advice.

Frequently asked questions

Is food selectivity always sensory?

No. Medical, nutritional, feeding-skill and psychosocial factors may contribute and can coexist.

Can I thicken drinks at home?

Not without guidance: texture changes can have consequences and should follow individual assessment.

This article is for information only and does not replace an assessment by a health professional. If you are worried about a child’s development, talk to their doctor or paediatrician.
