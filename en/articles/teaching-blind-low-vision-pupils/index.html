Blindness and low vision: teaching beyond what can be seen requires attention to the specific barriers between the pupil, the task and the environment. A diagnosis can guide questions, but it does not replace knowing the child, their strengths, communication and existing supports.

What is preventing access to learning?

Before assuming a lack of effort, check language, sensory or motor access, memory load, predictability and response demands. Observe a real task and listen to the pupil and family before choosing a purposeful adjustment.

Concrete classroom strategies

  • State the goal and show a completed example before work begins.
  • Break the task into short steps, check understanding and allow processing time.
  • Offer more than one accessible way to participate and show learning.

How can the team tell whether support is working?

Set an observable goal, record participation and work quality, and review support with the pupil. A useful adjustment improves access and agency; it should not automatically lower expectations or separate the child.

In short

  • Barriers arise between pupil, task and environment.
  • Effective supports are specific, taught, observed and reviewed.

What we can say

What we cannot say

  • A diagnosis alone does not prescribe one teaching method.
  • No classroom strategy guarantees a particular developmental outcome.

Frequently asked questions

Must the school wait for a diagnosis before adapting teaching?

No. Schools can remove identified barriers and monitor the effect without turning educational observation into diagnosis.

Does the same strategy work for every child?

No. It should be chosen with the child, family and team, tried in a real task and adjusted in light of results.

How should the pupil be involved?

Ask what helps, offer realistic choices and review the solution after trying it.

This article is for information only and does not replace an assessment by a health professional. If you are worried about a child’s development, talk to their doctor or paediatrician.
